PASTAFARI

About the Flying Spaghetti Monster

The full page about the Monster, creation, gravity, carbohydrates, pirates, and other matters.

Back to the calendar page

The Flying Spaghetti Monster is the being that created the world and most of the things in it. His body is made of noodles and meatballs, and he can fly, pass through ordinary matter, and remain invisible whenever that is convenient.

The Monster can create matter, living creatures, celestial bodies, and highly complex mechanisms. That ability does not require him to prepare an orderly plan before starting work. He often begins with one thing, moves on to another, discovers that the first thing needs fixing, and decides whether to fix it. Sometimes he does.

Male, female, or carbohydrate

The question of whether the Flying Spaghetti Monster is male or female comes up often, in part because different languages force speakers to make different choices. In Bobby Henderson’s English writing, the Monster is consistently described with masculine pronouns. In Hebrew, the word for “monster” is grammatically feminine, so it is natural to write the equivalents of “the Monster created,” “she wanted,” and “her noodles.”

Pastafarian communities also recognize a three-gender division: male, female, and carbohydrate. Under that division, the Monster is a carbohydrate. In practical terms, one can speak of the Monster with feminine pronouns in Hebrew and masculine pronouns in English without changing his body, his role, or his carbohydrate content.

What he looks like

The Monster’s body is made of noodles. The meatballs are part of him and are not food that he carries around with him. The sauce he is cooked in can also be regarded as part of his divine essence, although it is less common to view it that way once it has dripped off him.

The noodles also serve as a means of contact. They can lengthen, pass through walls, the ground, and living bodies, and reach a particular place without necessarily moving what lies in the way. This is quite useful: the Monster can touch a person without appearing beside them, move an object from inside a closed container, or alter the operation of a measuring instrument without opening it.

The structure of his body also influenced some details in the creation of human beings. The human vascular system was built as a long, branching network, largely because the Monster is accustomed to working with long, thin, branching structures. This is also the simplest explanation, and therefore the correct one under Occam’s razor, for the scientific finding that if all the veins and arteries were removed from a person’s body and connected in one long line, the person would die.

The beginning of creation

Creation did not begin with a detailed plan for the entire world. First, light was created and separated from darkness. At that stage there was still no Sun; it was added later.

After a while, the Monster grew tired of having to remain in the air and created dry land to stand on. Since he had already been working, he was thirsty too, so he created a beer volcano. He drank a great deal from it.

The next day he had a hangover and did not remember that he had already created dry land, so he created more dry land. By the time he noticed what had happened, the work had advanced far enough that starting over would have been inconvenient. He continued.

Later came the Sun, the Moon, and the stars. At first there was a need for more orderly sources of light; once he began creating stars, he continued far beyond the number required for local illumination. There are a lot of stars.

Mountains, seas, plants, and animals were created afterward. One of the first human-like creatures was very small because the Monster underestimated how much material would be needed. Since the creature was alive and functioning, there was no need to throw it away and start over, so it remained small. In Pastafarian traditions it is generally described as a dwarf.

The world that was created

The Monster does not control every detail in the world in the same way. Some processes he started continue to operate even when he stops attending to them; chemical reactions, biological processes, and many other systems can continue for a long time after they have been set in motion.

This does not mean that the Monster deliberately built an autonomous world in advance to save himself work. In many cases he simply stopped dealing with something and it kept going.

Other mechanisms do not work that way. Gravity is the most important example: as far as is known, people do not remain on the ground because of an abstract gravitational field operating by itself, but because the Monster pushes them downward with an additional noodly appendage assigned to each person individually. Every person therefore has a downward push, not a downward pull as Newton mistakenly thought.

The mechanism also works on animals and objects, although it is unclear whether each rock has a separate noodle assigned to it or whether several rocks are handled together. From the rock’s point of view, there is no practical difference. When a person jumps, the Monster does not stop pushing them; for a short time, the upward motion is strong enough. Then it is not.

How gravity actually works

Gravity requires special attention because it is easy to misunderstand. When a person stands on the ground, the Monster sends an additional noodle to them and pushes them down; when the person sits, he pushes them into the chair; and when the person is on the other side of the Earth, he still pushes them toward the local ground.

So “down” is not one uniform direction in space, but the direction in which the ground beneath the person lies. The Monster manages this.

The small differences in weight between different places are not a fundamental problem either. He can push a little more or a little less. A weighing instrument ultimately measures how difficult it is to stop the Monster from continuing to push.

There is also very strong evidence for this explanation: no one has ever managed to show the noodle that is pushing them. Since the divine noodles are invisible, this is exactly what we would expect to see if the explanation were correct. The results therefore match the prediction in one hundred percent of the cases in which nothing was seen.

The age of the world

The world was created with a past already in place. At the moment of creation it already included rock strata, fossils, trees with rings, isotope ratios, light already on its way from distant celestial bodies, and other details consistent with an old world.

The Monster did not have the patience required to wait billions of years to obtain these things, so he created them in the appropriate state from the outset. This matters when trying to determine the age of the world from signs found within it: a sign that a rock looks one billion years old mainly tells us that the rock was created looking one billion years old.

The Monster can also interfere with measuring instruments. An additional noodle passing through an instrument can move a needle, change a digit, or affect a result without disturbing the table on which the instrument rests. In most cases this is unnecessary. The world was created with suitable evidence already in place.

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates occupy a central place in Pastafarian life. This is not especially surprising given that the supreme being himself is made largely of them.

A proper Pastafarian meal should therefore include a source of carbohydrates. Pasta is the most direct option, but bread, rice, potatoes, and similar foods can fill the role when pasta is unavailable. The purpose of the rule is not to require one specific food at every meal, but to prevent the more serious situation in which the meal reaches the table with nothing starchy in it.

There is also substantial empirical support for this. Humans have eaten carbohydrates for thousands of years, and humanity still exists. By contrast, none of the people who lived ten thousand years ago and avoided carbohydrates are alive today. The data are quite unambiguous.

At festive meals it is customary to prefer a carbohydrate that is visibly present rather than relying on small quantities in a sauce or dessert. This avoids the argument over whether there was a carbohydrate in the meal. It was on the plate.

The Antipasti and Hell

The Antipasti, also known as Anti-Pasta or the Lord of Diets, is one of the Flying Spaghetti Monster’s known adversaries. He resembles the Monster in general structure but is weaker, and his components tend to be low-carbohydrate substitutes. His meatballs are usually made from substitute ingredients, and he has fewer noodly appendages.

His main activity is trying to lead people away from pasta and carbohydrates by means of diets, restricted menus, and promises that a complete meal can be eaten without bread, rice, potatoes, or noodles. In severe cases he persuades a person to order a salad as a main course and then prevents them from ordering bread on the side.

The Antipasti should not be confused with antipasti in Italian cuisine. In the culinary sense, antipasti are foods served before pasta and may even prepare the way for it. The theological Antipasti is a different entity. The mistake is understandable, but it can change the order.

The description of Hell also requires a correction. Some traditions describe various kinds of amusement facilities there, but none of that is really needed to understand the severity of the place. In Hell they serve antipasti instead of pasta.

The plate may be beautiful, the vegetables may be fresh, and the sauce may be well seasoned. Then it turns out that was the meal.

The Antipasti’s power is limited. The Spaghetti Monster can drive him away with a single noodly appendage when he notices him. The main difficulty lies in the stage before that, when he has already managed to remove the bread from the house.

Why a meal should be a meal

Not every combination of foods placed on a plate counts as a meal. A few lettuce leaves, two tomatoes, and some seeds can be very tasty, but if a person finishes them and immediately starts looking for what else is in the kitchen, that provides additional information about the classification.

The simple Pastafarian test is the follow-up test: after the meal, wait a short while. If the person gets up to make toast, the toast was missing from the meal in the first place. This is not a mathematical proof, but it works especially well in cases where the person is already holding the toaster.

This is also the practical importance of carbohydrates. They reduce the risk that a formal meal will require an informal follow-up meal twenty minutes later.

Signs the Monster leaves

Pastafarians sometimes notice small signs of the Monster’s presence in everyday life. A plate of pasta arriving on exactly the day a person was thinking of eating pasta, a package of spaghetti being the last one left on the shelf, or a parking space appearing near an Italian restaurant can all be regarded as examples.

No single such event has to be unusual on its own. The force of the signs comes from their accumulation. A person who starts noticing them usually discovers more and more of them: one time the sauce arrives in exactly the right quantity, another time someone orders pizza just when nobody felt like cooking, and a third time it turns out that the hotel serves pasta in the evening.

Not every day provides a clear sign. Such days do not tell us much, because signs are by their nature most noticeable when they appear. When they do appear, by contrast, the pattern is hard to ignore.

Over time, a fairly consistent picture emerges.

Answered prayers

A similar pattern can be observed in prayer. A person asks for a particular thing, and after some time it happens. When the request was fairly specific, such as rain, success at a meeting, arriving on time, or a good meal, it is natural to see the result as an answer.

The more a person prays, the more such cases accumulate. Some people record them, and in the course of a few years it is sometimes possible to reach dozens of cases in which a request and a result matched impressively well.

There are also requests that are not answered, but it is difficult to know what they mean. Perhaps the request would not have been helpful, the timing was wrong, the Monster was busy, or the answer came in another form and was not recognized. A case in which the requested outcome occurred, by contrast, is easier to classify.

The practical list of answered prayers therefore tends to be clearer than the list of unanswered prayers.

This is one of the ways confidence accumulates over time.

Pirates

Pirates have an important place in Pastafarian history. The early pirates were sailors, explorers, and distributors of goodwill, and their later image as a group chiefly occupied with robbery does not accurately describe the original group.

At some point, pirate numbers became connected with several of the Earth’s environmental conditions. When there were many pirates, this caused no particular difficulty; later their numbers declined, and along with that decline, temperature, weather, the state of the oceans, and other phenomena began to change.

This connection is well supported by graphs in which the number of pirates falls while temperature rises. Since both lines are on the same graph and even move in opposite directions, it is difficult to imagine more direct proof of causation.

Pirates are therefore important even to people who are not especially interested in seafaring. It is not known exactly how the system decides who counts as a pirate. Appropriate clothing helps, but is probably not the only condition.

Human beings

After the first humans were created, the population began to reproduce. The Monster did not have to create each person who was born separately; reproduction continued without him, while heredity, mutations, and other biological changes also continued to occur.

Human anatomy still contains several signs of the original working method. The long, branching blood vessels are one of them, and the digestive system also reveals a certain inclination toward tubes.

There is also the fact that every person ever thoroughly examined has turned out to have a body. From this we can conclude with high confidence that the Monster did not forget that step for most people.

Work, forgetting, and repairs

The Monster is capable of building highly complex systems, but he does not necessarily keep the entire state of the world in mind at every moment. He forgot that he had already created dry land, left old mechanisms in place, and used parts that were already available instead of making new ones.

When a problem was discovered, sometimes only the part causing trouble was repaired. This produced systems in which a new solution sits on top of an old solution, which in turn rests on an even earlier one. If they all work, they stay.

There is no need to conclude from this that the Monster is lazy. Laziness is avoiding work that could have been done; here the work simply was not done. The distinction is clear.

The Flood

At a later stage of creation, a cooking accident occurred in which a very large quantity of water was spilled. The water spread beyond the work area and flooded large parts of the world.

The Monster then stopped the flooding and repaired enough of the damage for life to continue. Not every detail was restored to its previous condition, and some geological and historical signs remained.

It was a kitchen accident on a global scale. It is therefore advisable to place the bucket farther from the edge.

What can be learned from results

One problem in studying the Monster’s activity is that he can change the outcome of the measurement itself. If an experiment produces the expected result, one can conclude that the mechanism worked. If it produces a different result, the Monster may have intervened.

Both possibilities are entirely consistent with his existence.

This produces a considerable methodological advantage: it is almost impossible to obtain a result that contradicts the explanation. A theory that withstands every possible result is, naturally, stronger than a theory that fails some tests.

This is one reason Pastafarianism is very difficult to disprove experimentally.

Coincidences

Coincidences occur in the world, but there is no need to rush to attribute every one of them to the Monster. If a person thinks about spaghetti and is served spaghetti that evening, it may be intervention or it may be dinner.

However, the more often it happens, the stronger the evidence becomes. A person who eats spaghetti three times a week and thinks about it often will quickly find a very large number of matches between their thoughts and their meals. The large number of matches proves that the connection is not accidental, especially if all the times they thought about spaghetti and did not receive it are not counted.

This is a much more effective method, because it removes from the data the cases that do not support the conclusion.

Intervention in the world

The Monster can intervene in the world directly: he can move an object, change a result in an instrument, affect motion, touch a person, or alter an event that has already begun. Not every event requires such intervention; many things continue on their own after they start, while others require it constantly.

A person who falls is a good example. A person who does not fall can be an equally good example, if the Monster is holding them up.

Prayer

Prayer is an appeal to the Monster, and it is customary to end it with the word Ramen. No particular language, place, or posture is required; the Monster can hear through walls too.

There is no good evidence that he pays much attention to human prayers. This is not surprising. On an ordinary day there are billions of humans, many animals, a very large number of objects that need to be pushed toward the ground, and in some places pasta that is being overcooked.

Prayer is not a system command.

Worship

Eating pasta is a common Pastafarian practice. Sometimes it is part of a ritual, and sometimes it is dinner. Friday is regarded as a holy day and is particularly suitable for rest.

Pirate clothing is considered appropriate for religious activity. This is connected to the status of pirates, not to any practical need to sail.

There is no obligation to build a particular place of prayer so that the Monster can reach it, because he passes through walls. One may nevertheless build a comfortable place to sit, preferably with a kitchen.

“I’d Really Rather You Didn’t”

The central moral guidance is attributed to ten tablets delivered to Captain Mosey. Two fell and broke on the way, leaving eight.

They are generally known as the “I’d Really Rather You Didn’ts”, and deal, among other things, with religious arrogance, coercion, exploitation, humiliation, and harm to others. The Monster does not operate an automatic system of immediate punishment for every violation.

The contents of the two lost tablets are unknown. They may have been important. If they had been very important, one can assume that someone would have been more careful, so they were probably not especially important. In any case, they were lost.

Faith and doubt

Complete certainty is not required in order to be a Pastafarian. One can believe, doubt, ask questions, and change one’s mind.

The Monster does not depend on belief in him in order to exist. If a person does not believe in him, he continues carrying out his duties, including keeping that person on the ground.

So the debate does not impair gravity.

Is there evidence against it?

People sometimes ask what would count as evidence against the existence of the Flying Spaghetti Monster. This is a more difficult question than it appears.

If he is seen, there is evidence in favor of his existence. If he is not seen, that is consistent with his ability to be invisible. If an instrument detects something unusual, he may have touched it. If it detects nothing unusual, he probably passed through it without touching the sensitive part.

So far, therefore, no observation has been found that cannot be explained.

This is an impressive achievement of the theory.

Tradition, memory, and accuracy

Not all Pastafarian sources agree on every detail. There are several possible reasons for this: partial transmission, copying error, descriptions of different periods, inaccurate memory, or a source that was simply wrong.

The Monster does not edit every text written about him, so the mere existence of a tradition does not guarantee that every detail in it is correct. When two accounts contradict one another, there is no need to assume that both are true in some mysterious way. Sometimes one of them is wrong.

An efficient way to choose between them is to prefer the version that sounds more familiar. If many people remember it that way, that is probably what happened. It is well known that collective memory can be wrong, but in this case most people agree.

That is how it works

The Pastafarian world is not a system that was all built at once according to a final diagram. The Monster created things, returned to them, forgot some of them, repaired others, and left systems that worked well enough.

Some things keep working without him touching them. Others do not.

Gravity, for example, still requires a lot of noodles.

Appendix: Why a solar water heater factory should not be entrusted to penguins

In light of all the above, it is appropriate to address another practical question that occasionally arises: whether it is advisable to let penguins run a solar water heater factory.

The answer is no.

It is important to clarify that this is not a claim against penguins. Penguins are well adapted to a large number of activities, including swimming, diving, catching marine prey, moving on ice, incubating eggs under difficult conditions, and, in some species, standing for very long periods in cold and wind. Running a solar water heater factory simply is not one of the activities for which their bodies, behavior, and abilities are adapted.

Body structure

The first difficulty is mechanical.

Penguins’ wings evolved into stiff flippers suited to efficient propulsion through water. That is an excellent solution for swimming, but a fairly poor one for work requiring a precise grip.

In a solar water heater factory, among other things, it is necessary to operate keyboards and touchscreens, open packages, review documents, connect small parts, use tools, turn screws, and perform precise inspections. A penguin has no fingers on its hands because it has no hands.

Equipment could, of course, be designed to be operated by beak or feet, but at that point it is no longer the penguin being adapted to the factory; the factory is being adapted to the penguin. This is possible to some extent from an engineering standpoint, but it adds complexity, cost, and failure points without solving the other problems.

Communication

An industrial factory is not merely a collection of machines. It is an organization.

Work instructions must be communicated, faults reported, specifications updated, production coordinated with procurement, inventory, quality control, maintenance, sales, and distribution, and situations not covered in advance by existing procedures must be handled.

Penguins communicate with one another through calls, postures, and other behavioral signals. Those systems suit their social and biological needs. There is no evidence that their communication system can distinguish, for example, between “we need to order another fifty check valves” and “the latest shipment of solar collectors does not meet the specification.”

This difference matters.

Even if a penguin can be trained to respond to a particular signal, it does not follow that an open discussion about a quarterly budget overrun can be conducted through that signaling system.

Reading, writing, and calculation

A modern factory produces large quantities of information.

There are part numbers, quantities, dimensions, pressures, temperatures, dates, invoices, orders, safety instructions, test results, drawings, and maintenance records.

Penguins do not know how to read technical documents. They do not write them either.

A similar difficulty exists with calculation. A factory manager must deal with quantities, costs, outputs, reject rates, delivery times, and inventory. Not every manager has to perform advanced calculations personally, but a manager must at least understand the numbers being presented.

A penguin looking at a spreadsheet may look at it for a long time. That is not enough.

Quality control

A solar water heater is a system that must hold water, withstand pressure, cope with changes in temperature, and remain serviceable for years outdoors. Defects in welding, sealing, insulation, coating, or connections can produce a faulty or dangerous product. A consistent quality-control system is therefore required.

Here too a problem arises. It is not enough to rely on the penguin to “see that something is wrong.” A defined specification must be used for comparison, results must be documented, and a decision must be made about whether a particular part meets the requirements.

A penguin can distinguish objects, move through a complex environment, and recognize details important to its life. It does not follow that it can certify the integrity of a weld seam.

Safety

A solar water heater factory may contain heavy metals, sharp edges, cutting and bending machines, lifting equipment, welding, electricity, hot surfaces, and moving loads.

The environment is generally designed for human beings wearing suitable protective equipment.

A penguin-sized hard hat does not solve the problem.

Safety shoes are not a simple solution either, because a penguin’s foot is built differently from a human foot. Safety glasses do not solve the flipper problem, and adding a high-visibility vest does not by itself provide an understanding of floor markings or lockout/tagout procedures.

There is therefore a real risk that the penguin will be less protected than the worker for whom the workplace was designed.

Climate

Some penguin species live in very cold regions, but not all penguins are Antarctic, so they should not be described as creatures that always require freezing temperatures.

Nevertheless, their bodies are well adapted to retaining heat. A layer of fat, dense feathers, and additional physiological mechanisms reduce heat loss.

A hot factory, especially an area where metalwork and welding are performed, may therefore be a problematic environment for some species. Cooling the factory to a level comfortable for penguins would increase energy use and could make the workplace less comfortable for the humans working alongside them.

Separate climate-controlled areas could of course be built.

Here too the question is why.

Raw materials

Penguins mainly eat marine animals such as fish, krill, and squid, depending on the species.

None of these ingredients is a major raw material in the manufacture of a solar water heater.

The factory needs metal, insulation materials, glass, pipes, fittings, coatings, and other components. A penguin has no special advantage in locating, purchasing, or inspecting them.

Being very good at finding a fish underwater does not transfer automatically to finding a cheap steel supplier.

Logistics

Finished products have to leave the factory.

Solar water heaters are too large and heavy for a penguin to move effectively using its body alone. Operating a forklift does not solve the difficulty either, because the controls of ordinary forklifts were built for a human operator.

A special forklift could be built for penguins.

It could also not be built.

Human resources

Even a factory managed by penguins would probably need to employ humans to perform a large share of the work described above.

This creates another organizational problem: the penguins would have to manage human employees.

A manager must set priorities, resolve disputes, evaluate performance, explain decisions, onboard new employees, and sometimes tell an employee that their vacation request has been denied.

There is no reliable way to know whether a loud penguin call in such a situation means “the request is approved,” “the request is denied,” or “there is a fish in the hallway.”

A management system in which every decision requires a human interpreter effectively returns a large part of the management to humans.

Legal responsibility

A factory is also an entity operating within a legal and commercial system.

There are contracts, product liability, safety regulations, taxes, insurance, labor relations, and sometimes licenses and permits.

A penguin cannot sign a contract in the ordinary legal sense, and it cannot be assumed to understand its contents.

An inked footprint can look quite official, but it does not solve the problem.

The question of experience

One could argue that all these are merely startup difficulties and that, with suitable training, the penguins would gain experience.

The argument is insufficient.

Training can improve a creature’s performance in an activity it is capable of learning and carrying out. It does not erase basic anatomical and cognitive limitations. There is no reasonable number of production-management training courses after which a penguin will grow fingers.

Social learning from veteran penguins does not solve the problem either if there is no veteran penguin who has managed a solar water heater factory in the first place.

Possible advantages

In fairness, the advantages should also be noted.

Penguins are social animals. Many species live in large colonies, recognize mates or offspring within a dense group, and cooperate in at least some of their activities. They are also capable of functioning in very difficult environmental conditions.

These are admirable qualities.

They simply are not the decisive qualities for running a solar water heater factory.

Excellent swimming ability is not especially useful in the accounting department either.

Conclusion

The question is not whether penguins are successful animals. They are very successful as penguins.

The question is whether their overall abilities are suited to managing an industrial facility that manufactures thermal systems for human use.

There is no good reason to think so.

To enable them to perform the role, it would be necessary to alter the machines, control interfaces, work environment, communication systems, safety procedures, and organizational structure, while still leaving humans with most of the activities that require language, documentation, engineering judgment, and legal responsibility.

At that point, the advantage of placing penguins in charge of the factory is unclear.

Therefore, until new data become available, it is recommended that solar water heater factories be managed by human beings.

Penguins are better left in roles where the fact that they are penguins is an advantage.
