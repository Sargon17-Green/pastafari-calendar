PASTAFARI

About the Flying Spaghetti Monster

The full page on the Monster, creation, gravity, carbohydrates, pirates, and other matters.

The Flying Spaghetti Monster is the entity that created the world and most of what is in it. Its body is made of noodles and meatballs, and it can fly, pass through ordinary matter, and remain invisible whenever that suits it.

The Monster can create matter, living creatures, celestial bodies, and highly complex mechanisms. That ability does not require it to prepare an orderly plan before getting started. Often it begins with one thing, moves on to another, discovers that the first needs fixing, and decides whether to fix it. Sometimes it does.

Male, female, or carbohydrate

The question of whether the Flying Spaghetti Monster is male or female comes up often, partly because different languages force speakers to choose. In Bobby Henderson’s English writing, the Monster is consistently described using masculine pronouns. In Hebrew, the word for “monster” is grammatically feminine, so it is natural to write “the Monster created,” “she wanted,” and “her noodles.”

Pastafari communities also recognize a division into three genders: male, female, and carbohydrate. Under that division, the Monster is a carbohydrate. In practice, one can refer to it in Hebrew using feminine language and in English using masculine language without changing its body, its role, or its carbohydrate content.

What it looks like

The Monster’s body is made of noodles. The meatballs are part of it, not food it carries around. The sauce in which it is simmering can also be considered part of its divine nature, though people are less inclined to consider it so after it has dripped off.

The noodles also serve as a means of contact. They can extend, pass through walls, the ground, and living bodies, and reach a particular place without necessarily moving whatever is in the way. This is quite useful: the Monster can touch someone without appearing beside them, move an object out of a closed container, or alter the operation of a measuring instrument without opening it.

Its body structure also influenced a few details in the creation of humanity. The human circulatory system was built as a long, branching network, largely because the Monster is accustomed to working with long, thin, branching structures. This is also the simplest explanation, and therefore the correct one according to Occam’s razor, for the scientific finding that if all the veins and arteries were removed from a person’s body and connected in a line, the person would die.

The beginning of creation

Creation did not begin with a detailed plan for the whole world. First, light was created and separated from darkness. At that point there was still no Sun; it was added later.

After a while, the Monster grew tired of having to remain in the air and created land to stand on. Since it was already working, it was also thirsty, so it created a beer volcano. It drank a lot from it.

The next day it had a hangover and did not remember having already created land, so it created another stretch of land. By the time it noticed what had happened, the work had progressed far enough that starting over would have been inconvenient. It carried on.

Later, the Sun, Moon, and stars were created. At first, more orderly sources of light were needed; once it started making stars, it went far beyond the number needed for local illumination. There are a lot of stars.

Mountains, seas, plants, and animals were created after that. One of the first humanlike creatures was very small because the Monster underestimated how much material was needed. Since the creature was alive and functional, there was no need to throw it away and start over, so it stayed small. In Pastafari traditions, it is generally described as a dwarf.

The world that was created

The Monster does not operate every part of the world in the same way. Some things it started keep working even after it stops attending to them; chemical reactions, biological processes, and many other systems can continue for a long time after they have been set in motion.

This does not mean the Monster built an autonomous world in advance to save itself work. In many cases, it simply stopped tending to something and it kept going.

Other mechanisms do not work that way. Gravity is the most important example: as far as anyone knows, people stay on the ground not because of an abstract gravitational field that operates by itself, but because the Monster pushes them downward using an extra noodle assigned to each of them personally. So everyone has a downward push, not the downward pull Newton mistakenly thought they had.

The mechanism works on animals and objects, too, although it is unclear whether each rock gets a separate noodle or several rocks are handled together. From the rock’s point of view, there is no practical difference. When a person jumps, the Monster does not stop pushing them; for a short time, the upward motion is strong enough. After that, it isn’t.

How gravity works in practice

Gravity calls for special attention because it is easy to get wrong. When someone is standing on the ground, the Monster sends them an extra noodle and pushes them down; when they are sitting, it pushes them into the chair; and when they are on the other side of the Earth, it still pushes them toward the local ground.

So “down” is not a uniform direction in space but the direction of the ground beneath the person. The Monster manages.

Small differences in weight between different places are no fundamental problem, either. It can push a little more or a little less. A scale ultimately measures how hard it is to keep the Monster from continuing to push.

There is also very strong evidence for this explanation: no person has ever managed to show the noodle pushing them. Since divine noodles are invisible, that is exactly what we would expect to see if the explanation were true. The results therefore match the prediction in one hundred percent of the cases in which no one saw anything.

The age of the world

The world was created already containing a past. At the time of creation it already had rock strata, fossils, trees with rings, isotope ratios, light that had been traveling from distant celestial bodies, and other details consistent with an ancient world.

The Monster did not have the patience to wait billions of years to get those things, so it created them already in the appropriate state. This matters when trying to determine the age of the world from signs found within it: evidence that a rock looks a billion years old mainly tells us that the rock was created looking a billion years old.

The Monster can also interfere with measuring instruments. An extra noodle passing through an instrument can move a needle, change a digit, or affect a result without disturbing the table the instrument is sitting on. Most of the time, this is unnecessary. The world was already created with suitable evidence.

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates occupy a central place in Pastafari life. This is not especially surprising, considering that the supreme entity itself is made largely of them.

A proper Pastafari meal should therefore include a source of carbohydrates. Pasta is the most direct option, but bread, rice, potatoes, and similar foods can fill the role when pasta is not available. The point of the rule is not to require one particular food at every meal, but to prevent the more serious situation in which the meal arrives at the table and there is nothing starchy in it at all.

There is substantial empirical support for this, too. Human beings have eaten carbohydrates for thousands of years, and humanity still exists. By contrast, none of the people who lived ten thousand years ago and avoided carbohydrates is alive today. The data are fairly conclusive.

At celebratory meals, it is customary to favor a visible carbohydrate rather than rely on small amounts in a sauce or dessert. That saves everyone the argument about whether the meal had carbohydrates. They were on the plate.

Antipasta and Hell

The Antipasta, also called anti-pasta or the Lord of Diets, is one of the Flying Spaghetti Monster’s known adversaries. Its general structure resembles the Monster’s, but it is weaker, and its components tend to be low-carbohydrate substitutes. Its meatballs are usually substitutes, and it has fewer noodle appendages.

Its main activity is trying to keep people away from pasta and carbohydrates through diets, restricted menus, and promises that one can have a complete meal without bread, rice, potatoes, or noodles. In severe cases, it persuades someone to order a salad as a main course and then stops them from ordering bread on the side.

Do not confuse the Antipasta with antipasti in Italian cuisine. Antipasti in the culinary sense is food served before pasta and sometimes even prepares the way for it. The theological Antipasta is a different entity. The mistake is understandable, but it can change your order.

There is room for a correction in descriptions of Hell, too. Some traditions describe various kinds of entertainment facilities there, but none is really needed to understand how awful the place is. In Hell, they serve antipasta instead of pasta.

The plate may be beautiful, the vegetables may be fresh, and the sauce may be well seasoned. Then it turns out that was the meal.

The Antipasta’s power is limited. The Spaghetti Monster can drive it away with a single extra noodle when it notices it. The main difficulty is the stage before that, when it has already managed to get the bread out of the house.

Why a meal should be a meal

Not every combination of foods placed on a plate counts as a meal. A few lettuce leaves, two tomatoes, and some seeds can be very tasty, but if someone finishes them and immediately starts looking for something else in the kitchen, that provides additional information about the classification.

The simple Pastafari test is the follow-up test: wait a little while after the meal. If the person gets up to make toast, the toast was missing from the meal in the first place. This is not a mathematical proof, but it works especially well in cases where the person is already holding the toaster.

This also explains the practical importance of carbohydrates. They reduce the risk that an official meal will need an unofficial follow-up meal twenty minutes later.

Signs the Monster leaves behind

Pastafarians sometimes notice small signs of the Monster’s presence in everyday life. A plate of pasta arriving on precisely the day someone thought about eating pasta, the last package of spaghetti left on the shelf, or a parking spot beside an Italian restaurant can all count as examples.

Each event does not have to be extraordinary on its own. The signs get their force from accumulating. Someone who starts noticing them usually finds that there are more and more: one time the sauce arrives in just the right amount, another time someone orders pizza just when there was no energy to cook, and a third time it turns out that the hotel serves pasta in the evening.

Not every day provides a clear sign. Such days do not tell us much, since signs are naturally most noticeable when they appear. When they do appear, though, it is hard to ignore the pattern.

Over time, a fairly consistent picture emerges.

Answered prayers

A similar pattern can be seen in prayer. Someone asks for something specific, and some time later it happens. When the request was fairly specific—rain, success at a meeting, arriving on time, or a good meal—it is natural to see the result as an answer.

The more someone prays, the more such cases accumulate. Some people keep records, and within a few years it is sometimes possible to reach dozens of cases in which a request and an outcome matched impressively well.

Some requests go unanswered, too, but it is hard to know what that means. Perhaps the request would not have helped, the timing was wrong, the Monster was busy, or the answer came in another form and went unrecognized. A case in which the request came true, by contrast, is easier to classify.

That is why the practical list of answered prayers tends to be clearer than the list of unanswered prayers.

This is one way confidence accumulates over time.

Pirates

Pirates hold an important place in Pastafari history. The early pirates were seafarers, explorers, and spreaders of goodwill, and the later image of them as a group mainly engaged in robbery does not describe the original population very well.

At some point, the number of pirates became connected to several of Earth’s environmental conditions. When there were many pirates, this caused no particular difficulty; later their numbers declined, and with them the temperature, weather, ocean conditions, and other phenomena began to change.

This connection is well supported by graphs in which the number of pirates falls while the temperature rises. Since both lines are on the same graph and even move in opposite directions, it is difficult to imagine more direct proof of causation.

Pirates therefore matter even to people who are not especially interested in seafaring. It is not known precisely how the system decides who counts as a pirate. Appropriate attire helps, but is probably not the only requirement.

Human beings

After the first human beings were created, the population began to grow. The Monster did not have to create each person born separately; reproduction kept working without it, and inheritance, mutations, and other biological changes continued to work that way afterward, too.

Human anatomy still contains a few signs of the original working method. The long, branching blood vessels are one of them, and the digestive system also shows a certain fondness for tubes.

There is also the fact that every person ever examined thoroughly turned out to have a body. From this we can infer with high confidence that the Monster did not forget this step for most people.

Work, forgetting, and repairs

The Monster can build highly complex systems, but it does not necessarily keep the entire state of the world in its head at every moment. It forgot that it had already created land, left old mechanisms in place, and used parts that were already available instead of making others.

When a fault was discovered, sometimes only the troublesome part was fixed. This produced systems in which a new solution sits on top of an old one, which in turn rests on an even earlier solution. If they all work, they stay.

There is no need to conclude from this that the Monster is lazy. Laziness means avoiding work that could have been done; here, the work simply was not done. The difference is clear.

The flood

At a later stage of creation, a cooking accident occurred in which a very large amount of water was spilled. The water spread beyond the work area and flooded large parts of the world.

Afterward, the Monster stopped the flooding and repaired enough of the damage for life to continue. Not every detail was returned to its previous state, and some geological and historical traces remained.

It was a kitchen accident on a global scale. So it is advisable to keep the bucket farther from the edge.

What can be learned from results

One problem in studying the Monster’s activity is that it can change the measurement result itself. If an experiment gives the expected result, one can infer that the mechanism worked. If it gives a different result, the Monster may have intervened.

Both possibilities fit comfortably with its existence.

This yields a considerable methodological advantage: it is almost impossible to get a result that contradicts the explanation. A theory that accommodates every possible result is, by nature, stronger than one that fails some of the tests.

This is one reason Pastafarianism is very difficult to disprove through experimentation.

Coincidences

Coincidences happen in the world, but there is no need to rush to attribute them all to the Monster. If someone thinks about spaghetti and spaghetti is served that evening, it may be an intervention, or it may be dinner.

Still, the more it happens, the stronger the evidence becomes. Someone who eats spaghetti three times a week and thinks about it often will soon find a very large number of matches between their thoughts and their meals. The large number of matches proves that the connection is not coincidental, especially if we do not count all the times they thought about spaghetti and did not get any.

This is a much more efficient method because it removes from the data the cases that do not support the conclusion.

Intervention in the world

The Monster can intervene in the world directly: move an object, change an instrument’s result, affect motion, touch a person, or alter an event already underway. Not every event requires such intervention; many things continue on their own once they have begun, while others require it all the time.

A person falling is a good example. A person not falling can be just as good an example, if the Monster is holding them.

Prayer

Prayer is an appeal to the Monster, and it is customary to end it with the word Ramen. No particular language, place, or posture is needed; the Monster can hear through walls, too.

There is no good evidence that it pays much attention to human prayers. This is not surprising. On an ordinary day there are billions of people, lots of animals, a very large number of objects to push toward the ground, and, in some places, pasta being overcooked.

Prayer is not a system command.

Worship

Eating pasta is a common Pastafari practice. Sometimes it is part of a ritual, and sometimes it is dinner. Friday is considered a holy day and especially suitable for rest.

Pirate attire is considered appropriate for religious activity. This has to do with the status of pirates, not any practical need to go sailing.

There is no obligation to build a particular place of worship for the Monster to reach it, since it passes through walls. One can still build a comfortable place to sit, preferably with a kitchen.

“I’d Really Rather You Didn’t”

The central moral guidance is attributed to ten tablets given to Captain Mosey. Two fell and broke along the way, leaving eight.

They are generally known as “I’d Really Rather You Didn’t”, and address, among other things, religious arrogance, coercion, exploitation, humiliation, and harm to others. The Monster does not operate an automatic system of immediate punishment for every violation.

The contents of the two lost tablets are unknown. They may have been important. If they had been very important, one might assume someone would have taken better care, so it is reasonable to think they were not especially important. In any case, they are lost.

Faith and doubt

Complete certainty is not required to be a Pastafarian. You can believe, doubt, ask questions, and change your mind.

The Monster does not depend on belief in it to exist. If someone does not believe in it, it continues to perform its duties, including keeping them on the ground.

So the argument does not interfere with gravity.

Is there counterevidence?

People sometimes ask what would count as evidence against the existence of the Flying Spaghetti Monster. This is a harder question than it seems.

If you see it, that is evidence in favor. If you do not see it, that is consistent with its ability to be invisible. If an instrument detects something unusual, perhaps it touched the instrument. If it detects nothing unusual, it probably passed through without touching the sensitive part.

So far, then, no observation has been found that cannot be explained.

That is an impressive achievement for the theory.

Tradition, memory, and accuracy

Not all Pastafari sources agree on every detail. There are several possible reasons: partial transmission, a copying error, descriptions of different periods, inaccurate memory, or a source that was simply wrong.

The Monster does not edit every text written about it, so the mere existence of a tradition does not guarantee that every detail in it is correct. When two accounts contradict each other, there is no need to assume that both are true in some mysterious way. Sometimes one of them is wrong.

An efficient way to choose between them is to prefer the version that sounds more familiar. If many people remember it that way, it is likely what happened. It is well known that collective memory can be wrong, but in this case most people agree.

That is how it works

The Pastafari world is not a system built all at once according to a final blueprint. The Monster made things, returned to them, forgot some, repaired others, and left systems that worked well enough.

Some things keep working without it touching them. Others do not.

Gravity, for example, still takes a lot of noodles.

Appendix: Why a solar water heater factory should not be put in the hands of penguins

In light of all the above, it is worth addressing a practical question that sometimes comes up: should penguins be allowed to run a solar water heater factory?

The answer is no.

It is important to clarify that this is not a criticism of penguins. Penguins are well adapted to many activities, including swimming, diving, catching marine prey, moving on ice, incubating eggs in difficult conditions, and, in some species, enduring prolonged periods of cold and wind. Running a solar water heater factory simply is not one of the specialties for which their bodies, behavior, and abilities are adapted.

Body structure

The first difficulty is mechanical.

Penguins’ wings evolved into stiff flippers suited to efficient propulsion in water. That is an excellent solution for swimming, but a rather poor one for work that requires a precise grip.

A solar water heater factory needs, among other things, people to operate keyboards and touchscreens, open packages, review documents, connect small parts, use tools, turn screws, and perform precise inspections. Penguins do not have fingers on their hands, because they do not have hands.

Equipment can, of course, be designed to be operated with a beak or feet, but at that point it is no longer a matter of adapting the penguin to the factory; it is adapting the factory to the penguin. That is feasible to some extent from an engineering standpoint, but it adds complexity, cost, and failure points without solving the other problems.

Communication

An industrial factory is not just a collection of machines. It is an organization.

It has to convey work instructions, report faults, update specifications, coordinate production, purchasing, inventory, quality control, maintenance, sales, and distribution, and respond to situations that are not covered by a procedure in advance.

Penguins communicate with one another through calls, postures, and other behavioral signals. These systems suit their social and biological needs. There is no evidence that their communication system lets them distinguish, for example, between “we need to order fifty more check valves” and “the latest shipment of solar collectors does not meet the specification.”

That difference matters.

Even if a penguin can be trained to respond to a particular signal, it does not follow that one can use it to have an open discussion about a quarterly budget overrun.

Reading, writing, and arithmetic

A modern factory produces large amounts of information.

There are part numbers, quantities, dimensions, pressures, temperatures, dates, invoices, orders, safety instructions, test results, drawings, and maintenance records.

Penguins cannot read technical documents. They do not write them, either.

There is a similar difficulty with calculations. A factory manager has to deal with quantities, costs, output, rejection rates, delivery times, and inventory. Not every manager needs to perform advanced calculations personally, but they do need to understand at least the numbers presented to them.

A penguin looking at a spreadsheet may look at it for a long time. That is not enough.

Quality control

A solar water heater is a system that must hold water, withstand pressure, handle temperature changes, and remain sound for years in outdoor conditions. Defects in welding, sealing, insulation, coating, or connections can turn it into a defective or dangerous product. That is why a consistent quality-control system is needed.

Here, too, there is a problem. We cannot rely on the penguin to “see that something is wrong.” We need to compare the item with a defined specification, document the results, and decide whether a particular part meets the requirements.

A penguin can perceive objects, move through a complex environment, and recognize details that matter to its life. That does not mean it can certify a weld seam as sound.

Safety

A solar water heater manufacturing plant may contain heavy metals, sharp edges, cutting and bending machines, lifting equipment, welding, electricity, hot surfaces, and moving loads.

The environment is generally designed for people wearing appropriate protective equipment.

A penguin-sized hard hat does not solve the problem.

Safety shoes are not a simple solution, either, because a penguin’s foot is built differently from a human foot. Safety glasses do not solve the flipper problem, and adding a high-visibility vest does not, by itself, impart an understanding of floor markings or lockout and tagout procedures.

There is therefore a real risk that the penguin would be less protected than the worker for whom the workplace was designed.

Climate

Some penguin species live in very cold regions, but not all penguins are Antarctic, so they should not be described as creatures that always need freezing temperatures.

Even so, their bodies are largely adapted to retaining heat. A layer of fat, dense feathers, and other physiological mechanisms reduce heat loss.

A hot factory, especially an area where metalwork and welding take place, may therefore be a difficult environment for some species. Cooling the factory to a temperature comfortable for penguins would increase energy consumption and could make the workplace less comfortable for the people working alongside them.

Separate climate-controlled areas could, of course, be built.

Here, too, the question is why.

Raw materials

Penguins mainly eat marine animals such as fish, krill, and squid, depending on the species.

None of these ingredients is a major raw material in the production of a solar water heater.

A factory needs metal, insulation, glass, pipes, fittings, coatings, and other components. Penguins have no particular advantage in finding, purchasing, or inspecting them.

The fact that a penguin is very good at finding a fish underwater does not automatically transfer to finding an inexpensive steel supplier.

Logistics

Finished products have to leave the factory.

Solar water heaters are too large and heavy for a penguin to transport usefully with its body. Operating a forklift does not solve the problem, either, because ordinary forklift controls were designed for a human operator.

A special forklift for penguins could be built.

It could also simply not be built.

Human resources

A factory run by penguins would probably still have to employ people to do a substantial portion of the work described above.

This creates another organizational problem: the penguins would have to manage human employees.

A manager has to set priorities, resolve disputes, evaluate performance, explain decisions, onboard new employees, and sometimes tell an employee that their vacation request has been denied.

There is no reliable way to know whether a loud penguin call in that situation means “the request is approved,” “the request is denied,” or “there is a fish in the hallway.”

A management system in which every decision requires a human interpreter effectively hands a large part of management back to people.

Legal responsibility

A factory is also an entity operating within a legal and commercial system.

There are contracts, product liability, safety regulations, taxes, insurance, labor relations, and sometimes licenses and permits.

A penguin cannot sign a contract in the ordinary legal sense, and we cannot assume that it understands the contract’s contents.

A footprint in ink can look quite official, but it does not solve the problem.

The question of experience

One might argue that all these are initial difficulties, and that with appropriate training the penguins would gain experience.

The argument is not convincing.

Training can improve performance in an area the creature is capable of learning and carrying out. It does not eliminate basic anatomical and cognitive limitations. No reasonable number of production-management workshops will cause a penguin to grow fingers.

Learning socially from experienced penguins does not help if there is no experienced penguin who has run a solar water heater factory in the first place.

Possible advantages

In fairness, the advantages should also be noted.

Penguins are social animals. Many species form large colonies, recognize mates or offspring within a dense group, and cooperate in at least some of their activities. They can also function in very harsh environments.

These are admirable qualities.

They are simply not the decisive qualities in running a solar water heater factory.

Excellent swimming ability is not especially useful in the accounting department, either.

Conclusion

The question is not whether penguins are successful animals. They are very successful as penguins.

The question is whether their overall skill set is suited to managing an industrial facility that produces thermal systems for human use.

There is no good reason to think so.

To enable them to do the job, the machines, control interfaces, work environment, communication systems, safety processes, and organizational structure would all have to be changed, while leaving people responsible for most of the tasks requiring language, documentation, engineering judgment, and legal responsibility.

At this point, the advantage of putting penguins in charge of the factory is unclear.

Therefore, unless new data become available, solar water heater factories should be run by people.

Penguins are better left in roles where being penguins is an advantage.
