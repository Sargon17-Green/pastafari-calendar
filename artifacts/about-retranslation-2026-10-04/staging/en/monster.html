PASTAFARI

About the Flying Spaghetti Monster

The full page about the Flying Spaghetti Monster, creation, gravity, carbohydrates, pirates, and other matters.

Back to the calendar page

The Flying Spaghetti Monster is the being who created the world and most of the things in it. Her body is made of noodles and meatballs, and she can fly, pass through ordinary matter, and remain invisible when it suits her.

The Flying Spaghetti Monster can create matter, living creatures, celestial bodies, and very complex mechanisms. This ability does not require her to prepare an orderly plan before starting work. Often she starts with one thing, moves on to another, discovers that the first needs fixing, and decides whether to fix it. Sometimes she does.

Male, Female, or Carbohydrate

The question of whether the Flying Spaghetti Monster is male or female comes up often, partly because different languages force speakers to choose. In Bobby Henderson’s English writing, the Monster is consistently described with masculine pronouns. In Hebrew, the word “monster” is grammatically feminine, so it is natural to write “the Monster created,” “she wanted,” and “her noodles.”

Pastafarian communities also commonly recognize three genders: male, female, and carbohydrate. According to this classification, the Monster is a carbohydrate. In practice, feminine pronouns in Hebrew and masculine pronouns in English change neither the Monster’s body, her role, nor her carbohydrate content.

What She Looks Like

The Monster’s body is made of noodles. The meatballs are part of her, not food she carries around with her. The sauce she simmers in can also be considered part of her divine nature, though it is less commonly considered so after it has dripped off her.

The noodles also serve as a means of contact. They can lengthen, pass through walls, ground, and living bodies, and reach a particular place without necessarily moving whatever is in the way. This is quite useful: the Monster can touch someone without appearing beside them, move an object out of a closed container, or alter the operation of a measuring device without opening it.

The structure of her body also influenced a few details in the creation of humanity. The human circulatory system was built as a long, branching network, largely because the Monster is accustomed to working with long, thin, branching structures. This is also the simplest explanation, and therefore the correct one according to Occam’s razor, for the scientific finding that if all the veins and arteries were removed from a person’s body and connected in a single line, the person would die.

The Beginning of Creation

Creation did not begin with a detailed plan for the entire world. First, light was created and separated from darkness. At that point there was still no sun; it was added later.

After a while, the Monster grew tired of having to stay in the air and created dry land on which it would be possible to stand. Since she was already working, she was also thirsty, so she created a beer volcano. She drank a lot of it.

The next day she had a hangover and did not remember that she had already created dry land, so she created another one. By the time she noticed what had happened, the work had progressed far enough that starting over would have been inconvenient. She carried on.

Later, the sun, moon, and stars were created. At first, more orderly sources of light were needed; after she began creating stars, she kept going well beyond the number needed for local illumination. There are a lot of stars.

Mountains, seas, plants, and animals came next. One of the first humanlike creatures was very small, because the Monster underestimated how much material was needed. Since the creature was alive and functioning, there was no need to throw it away and start over, so it stayed small. In Pastafarian traditions, it is usually described as a dwarf.

The World That Was Created

The Monster does not operate every detail of the world in the same way. Some things she started keep working even after she stops dealing with them; chemical reactions, biological processes, and many other systems can continue long after they have been set in motion.

This does not mean that the Monster built an autonomous world in advance to save herself work. In many cases, she simply stopped attending to something and it kept going.

Other mechanisms do not work that way. Gravity is the most important example: as far as we know, people do not stay on the ground because of an abstract gravitational field operating on its own, but because the Monster pushes them downward with a personal noodle attachment assigned to each of them. Every person therefore gets a push downward, not a pull downward, as Newton mistakenly thought.

The mechanism also works on animals and objects, although it is unclear whether a separate noodle is assigned to every stone or whether several stones are handled together. From the stone’s point of view, there is no practical difference. When a person jumps, the Monster does not stop pushing them; for a short while, the upward motion is strong enough. After that, it is not.

How Gravity Works in Practice

Gravity requires special attention because it is easy to get wrong. When a person stands on the ground, the Monster sends them a noodle attachment and pushes them down; when they sit, she pushes them into the chair; and when they are on the other side of the planet, she still pushes them toward the local ground.

So “down” is not a single direction in space, but the direction of the ground beneath the person. The Monster manages.

Small differences in weight between different places are not a fundamental problem either. She can push a little more or a little less. A device that measures weight ultimately measures how hard it is to stop the Monster from continuing to push.

There is also very strong evidence for this explanation: no person has ever managed to show the noodle pushing them. Since divine noodles are invisible, that is exactly what we would expect to see if the explanation were true. The results therefore match the prediction in 100 percent of the cases where nothing was seen.

The Age of the World

The world was created with a past already included. At the time of creation it already had layers of rock, fossils, trees with rings, isotope ratios, light that had been traveling from distant celestial bodies, and other details consistent with an ancient world.

The Monster did not have the patience to wait billions of years to get these things, so she created them already in the appropriate state. This matters when trying to determine the age of the world from signs found within it: evidence that a rock looks a billion years old mostly shows that the rock was created looking a billion years old.

The Monster can also interfere with measuring devices. A noodle attachment passing through a device can move a needle, change a digit, or affect a result without disturbing the table the device is resting on. Most of the time, there is no need. The world was already created with suitable evidence.

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates occupy a central place in Pastafarian life. This is not especially surprising, considering that the supreme being herself is made largely of them.

A proper Pastafarian meal should therefore include a source of carbohydrates. Pasta is the most direct option, but bread, rice, potatoes, and similar foods can also do the job when pasta is unavailable. The point of the rule is not to require one particular food at every meal, but to prevent the more serious situation in which a meal arrives at the table with nothing starchy in it at all.

There is also considerable empirical support for this. Humans have eaten carbohydrates for thousands of years, and humanity still exists. By contrast, none of the people who lived ten thousand years ago and avoided carbohydrates is alive today. The data are fairly conclusive.

At celebratory meals, it is customary to prefer a visible carbohydrate rather than rely on small amounts in the sauce or dessert. This avoids the argument over whether there was a carbohydrate in the meal. It was on the plate.

Antipasti and Hell

The Antipasta, also known as Anti-Pasta or the Lord of Diets, is one of the Flying Spaghetti Monster’s well-known adversaries. Its general structure is similar to hers, but it is weaker, and its components tend to be low-carbohydrate substitutes. Its meatballs are usually made from a substitute, and it has fewer noodle attachments.

Its main activity is trying to turn people away from pasta and carbohydrates with diets, restricted menus, and promises that a full meal can be eaten without bread, rice, potatoes, or noodles. In severe cases, it persuades someone to order a salad as a main course and then stops them from ordering bread on the side.

The Antipasta should not be confused with antipasti in Italian cuisine. Antipasti in the culinary sense is food served before pasta and sometimes even prepares the way for it. The theological Antipasta is a different entity. The mistake is understandable, but it can change the order.

There is also room for correction in descriptions of hell. Some traditions describe various kinds of entertainment facilities there, but none of that is really necessary to understand how dreadful the place is. In hell, they serve Antipasta instead of pasta.

The plate can be beautiful, the vegetables can be fresh, and the sauce can be seasoned just right. Then it turns out that was the meal.

The Antipasta’s power is limited. The Flying Spaghetti Monster can drive it away with a single noodle attachment when she notices it. The main difficulty is the stage before that, when it has already managed to get the bread out of the house.

Why a Meal Should Be a Meal

Not every combination of foods placed on a plate counts as a meal. A few lettuce leaves, two tomatoes, and some seeds can be very tasty, but if a person finishes them and immediately starts looking for something else in the kitchen, that provides additional information about the classification.

The simple Pastafarian test is the follow-up test: after the meal, wait a little while. If the person gets up to make toast, the toast was missing from the meal in the first place. This is not mathematical proof, but it works especially well in cases where the person is already holding the toaster.

This also explains the practical importance of carbohydrates. They reduce the risk that an official meal will need an unofficial follow-up meal twenty minutes later.

Signs the Flying Spaghetti Monster Leaves Behind

Pastafarians sometimes notice small signs of the Monster’s presence in everyday life. A plate of pasta arriving on exactly the day someone thought about eating pasta, the last package of spaghetti left on the shelf, or a parking spot found next to an Italian restaurant can all count as examples.

There is no need for each event to be extraordinary on its own. The signs get their strength from accumulating. Someone who starts paying attention to them usually finds that there are more and more: one time the sauce comes in exactly the right amount, another time someone orders pizza just when there was no energy to cook, and a third time it turns out the hotel serves pasta in the evening.

Not every day provides a clear sign. Days like that do not tell us much, because signs are by nature most noticeable when they appear. When they do appear, however, it is hard to ignore the pattern.

Over time, a fairly consistent picture emerges.

Answered Prayers

A similar pattern can be seen in prayer. Someone asks for something specific, and after a while it happens. When the request was fairly specific—rain, success at a meeting, arriving on time, or a good meal—it is natural to see the result as an answer.

The more a person prays, the more such cases accumulate. Some people keep records, and within a few years it is sometimes possible to reach dozens of cases in which request and outcome matched impressively well.

There are also requests that go unanswered, but it is hard to know what they mean. Perhaps the request would not have helped, the timing was wrong, the Monster was busy, or the answer came in another form and went unrecognized. A case where the request came true, by contrast, is easier to classify.

That is why the practical list of answered prayers tends to be clearer than the list of unanswered prayers.

This is one way confidence builds over time.

Pirates

Pirates have an important place in Pastafarian history. The ancient pirates were seafarers, explorers, and spreaders of good will, and the later image of them as a group mainly engaged in robbery does not accurately describe the original population.

At some point, the number of pirates became linked to several environmental conditions on Earth. When there were many pirates, this did not cause any particular difficulty; later their numbers fell, and along with them the temperature, weather, condition of the oceans, and other phenomena began to change.

This connection is well supported by graphs in which the number of pirates falls while the temperature rises. Since the two lines appear on the same graph and even move in opposite directions, it is hard to imagine more direct proof of causation.

Pirates are therefore important even to people who are not especially interested in seafaring. It is not known exactly how the system decides who counts as a pirate. Appropriate clothing helps, but it is probably not the only requirement.

Human Beings

After the first humans were created, the population began to grow. The Monster did not have to create every person born separately; reproduction kept working without her, and so did heredity, mutations, and other biological changes later on.

Human anatomy still contains a few signs of the original working method. The long, branching blood vessels are one of them, and the digestive system also shows a certain fondness for tubes.

There is also the fact that every person ever examined thoroughly turned out to have a body. From this, we can confidently conclude that the Monster did not forget that step for most people.

Work, Forgetfulness, and Repairs

The Monster can build very complex systems, but she does not necessarily keep the entire state of the world in her head at every moment. She forgot that she had already created dry land, left old mechanisms in place, and used parts that were already available instead of making others.

When a fault was discovered, sometimes only the part causing the problem was repaired. This produced systems in which a new solution sits on top of an old one, which in turn rests on an even earlier solution. If they all work, they stay.

There is no need to conclude from this that the Monster is lazy. Laziness means avoiding work that could have been done; here, the work simply was not done. The difference is clear.

The Flood

At a later stage of creation, a cooking accident occurred in which a very large amount of water was spilled. The water spread beyond the work area and flooded extensive parts of the world.

Afterward, the Monster stopped the flooding and repaired enough of the damage for life to continue. Not every detail was restored to its previous state, and some geological and historical signs remained.

It was a kitchen accident on a global scale. So it is recommended that you put the bucket farther from the edge.

What Can Be Learned from Results

One problem in studying the Monster’s activities is that she can change the measurement result itself. If an experiment produces the expected result, we can conclude that the mechanism worked. If it produces a different result, the Monster may have intervened.

Both possibilities fit her existence very well.

This yields a considerable methodological advantage: it is almost impossible to get a result that contradicts the explanation. A theory that survives every possible result is naturally stronger than a theory that fails some of the tests.

This is one reason Pastafarianism is very difficult to disprove by experiment.

Coincidences

Coincidences happen in the world, but there is no need to rush to attribute them all to the Monster. If someone thinks about spaghetti and spaghetti is served that evening, it may be an intervention, or it may be dinner.

Still, the more often it happens, the stronger the evidence becomes. Someone who eats spaghetti three times a week and thinks about it often will soon find a very large number of matches between their thoughts and their meals. The large number of matches proves that the connection is not random, especially if we do not count all the times they thought about spaghetti and did not get it.

This is a much more efficient method, because it removes from the data the cases that do not support the conclusion.

Intervention in the World

The Monster can intervene in the world directly: move an object, change a result on a device, affect movement, touch a person, or alter an event that has already begun. Not every event requires such intervention; many things continue on their own after they start, while others require it all the time.

A person falling is a good example. A person not falling can be just as good an example, if the Monster is holding them.

Prayer

Prayer is an appeal to the Monster, and it is customary to end it with the word Ramen. No particular language, place, or posture is required; the Monster can hear through walls too.

There is no good evidence that she pays much attention to human prayers. This is not surprising. On an ordinary day there are billions of people, many animals, a very large number of objects to push toward the ground, and in some places pasta that is being overcooked.

Prayer is not a system command.

Worship

Eating pasta is a customary Pastafarian practice. Sometimes it is part of a ritual and sometimes it is dinner. Friday is considered a holy day and especially suitable for resting.

Pirate clothing is considered appropriate for religious activity. This has to do with the status of pirates, not with any practical need to go sailing.

There is no requirement to build a particular place of worship for the Monster to reach, since she passes through walls. It is still possible to build a comfortable place to sit, and it should ideally have a kitchen.

“I’d Really Rather You Didn’t”

The central moral guidance is attributed to ten tablets given to Captain Mosey. Two fell and broke along the way, so eight remained.

They are generally known as “I’d Really Rather You Didn’t”, and address, among other things, religious arrogance, coercion, exploitation, humiliation, and harming others. The Monster does not operate an automatic system of immediate punishment for every violation.

The contents of the two lost tablets are unknown. They may have been important. If they had been very important, one can assume someone would have been more careful, so it is reasonable to conclude they were not especially important. In any case, they were lost.

Faith and Doubt

Complete certainty is not required to be a Pastafarian. One can believe, doubt, ask questions, and change one’s mind.

The Monster does not depend on belief in her in order to exist. If someone does not believe in her, she continues to perform her duties, including keeping them on the ground.

So the argument does not affect gravity.

Is There Contrary Evidence?

The question of what would count as evidence against the existence of the Flying Spaghetti Monster is sometimes raised. It is a harder question than it seems.

If you see her, that is evidence in favor. If you do not see her, that is consistent with her ability to be invisible. If a device detects something unusual, she may have touched it. If it detects nothing unusual, she probably passed through it without touching the sensitive part.

So far, then, no observation has been found that cannot be explained.

That is an impressive achievement for the theory.

Tradition, Memory, and Accuracy

Not all Pastafarian sources agree on every detail. There are several possible reasons: incomplete transmission, a copying error, descriptions of different periods, inaccurate memory, or a source that was simply wrong.

The Monster does not edit every text written about her, so the mere existence of a tradition does not guarantee that every detail in it is correct. When two accounts contradict each other, there is no need to assume that both are true in some mysterious way. Sometimes one of them is wrong.

An efficient way to choose between them is to prefer the version that sounds more familiar. If many people remember it that way, that is probably what happened. It is well known that collective memory can be wrong, but in this case most people agree.

That Is How It Works

The Pastafarian world is not a system built all at once according to a final diagram. The Monster created things, returned to them, forgot some, repaired others, and left systems that worked well enough.

Some things keep working without her touching them. Others do not.

Gravity, for example, still requires a lot of noodles.

Appendix: Why a Solar Water Heater Factory Should Not Be Entrusted to Penguins

In light of everything above, it is worth addressing a practical question that sometimes comes up: should penguins be allowed to run a solar water heater factory?

The answer is no.

It is important to clarify that this is not a criticism of penguins. Penguins are well suited to a large number of activities, including swimming, diving, catching marine prey, moving on ice, incubating eggs in harsh conditions, and, in some species, standing for a very long time in cold and wind. Running a solar water heater factory is simply not one of the specialties for which their bodies, behavior, and abilities are adapted.

Body Structure

The first difficulty is mechanical.

Penguins’ wings evolved into stiff flippers suited to efficient movement through water. This is an excellent solution for swimming, but a fairly poor one for work that requires precise gripping.

A solar water heater factory requires, among other things, operating keyboards and touchscreens, opening packages, reviewing documents, connecting small parts, using tools, turning screws, and performing precise inspections. Penguins do not have fingers on their hands, because they do not have hands.

It is possible to design equipment operated with the beak or feet, but at that point the penguin is not being adapted to the factory; the factory is being adapted to the penguin. This is somewhat possible from an engineering perspective, but it adds complexity, cost, and failure points without solving the other problems.

Communication

An industrial factory is not just a collection of machines. It is an organization.

Work instructions have to be communicated, faults reported, specifications updated, production coordinated with procurement, inventory, quality control, maintenance, sales, and distribution, and situations not covered by a procedure in advance have to be handled.

Penguins communicate with one another through calls, postures, and other behavioral signals. These systems suit their social and biological needs. There is no evidence that their communication system can distinguish, for example, between “order fifty more check valves” and “the last shipment of solar collectors does not meet the specifications.”

This difference matters.

Even if a penguin can be trained to respond to a particular signal, that does not mean an open discussion about a quarterly budget overrun can be conducted through one.

Reading, Writing, and Arithmetic

A modern factory produces large amounts of information.

There are part numbers, quantities, dimensions, pressures, temperatures, dates, invoices, orders, safety instructions, inspection results, drawings, and maintenance records.

Penguins cannot read technical documents. They do not write them either.

A similar difficulty arises with arithmetic. A factory manager has to deal with quantities, costs, output, rejection rates, delivery times, and inventory. Not every manager has to perform advanced calculations personally, but they at least need to understand the numbers presented to them.

A penguin looking at a spreadsheet may look at it for a long time. That is not enough.

Quality Control

A solar water heater is a system that needs to hold water, withstand pressure, cope with temperature changes, and remain in good condition for years outdoors. Defects in welding, sealing, insulation, coating, or connections can turn it into a defective or dangerous product. For this reason, a consistent quality-control system is needed.

Here, too, a problem arises. It is not possible to rely on the penguin “noticing that something is wrong.” The product has to be compared with a defined specification, the results documented, and a decision made about whether a particular component meets the requirements.

A penguin can recognize objects, move through a complex environment, and identify details that matter to its life. That does not mean it can approve the integrity of a weld seam.

Safety

A solar water heater factory may include heavy metals, sharp edges, cutting and bending machines, lifting equipment, welding, electricity, hot surfaces, and moving cargo.

The environment is generally designed for humans wearing appropriate protective equipment.

A penguin-sized hard hat does not solve the problem.

Safety shoes are not a simple solution either, because a penguin’s foot is built differently from a human foot. Safety glasses do not solve the flipper problem, and adding a high-visibility vest does not in itself provide an understanding of floor markings or lockout and tagout procedures.

There is therefore a real risk that the penguin would be less protected than the worker for whom the work environment was designed.

Climate

Some penguin species live in very cold regions, but not all penguins are Antarctic, so they should not be described as creatures that always need freezing temperatures.

Still, their bodies are largely adapted to retaining heat. A layer of fat, dense feathers, and other physiological mechanisms reduce heat loss.

A hot factory, especially an area where metalwork and welding take place, may therefore be a difficult environment for some species. Cooling the factory to a level comfortable for penguins would increase energy consumption and could make the working environment less comfortable for the humans working alongside them.

Separate climate-controlled areas could, of course, be built.

Here, too, the question is why.

Raw Materials

Penguins mainly eat marine animals such as fish, krill, and squid, depending on the species.

None of these ingredients is a major raw material in the production of a solar water heater.

The factory needs metal, insulating materials, glass, pipes, connectors, coatings, and other components. Penguins have no particular advantage in locating, purchasing, or inspecting them.

The fact that a penguin is very good at finding a fish underwater does not automatically transfer to finding a cheap steel supplier.

Logistics

Finished products have to leave the factory.

Solar water heaters are too large and heavy for a penguin to move usefully with its body. Operating a forklift does not solve the difficulty either, because ordinary forklift controls were designed for a human operator.

A special forklift could be built for penguins.

One could simply choose not to do that.

Human Resources

A factory run by penguins would most likely still need to employ humans to perform a substantial portion of the work described above.

This creates another organizational problem: the penguins would have to manage human employees.

A manager has to set priorities, resolve disagreements, evaluate performance, explain decisions, onboard new employees, and sometimes tell an employee that their vacation request has been denied.

There is no reliable way to know whether a loud penguin call in that situation means “request approved,” “request denied,” or “there is a fish in the hallway.”

A management system in which every decision requires a human interpreter would effectively leave a large part of management in human hands again.

Legal Liability

A factory is also an entity that operates within a legal and commercial system.

There are contracts, product liability, safety regulations, taxes, insurance, labor relations, and sometimes licenses and permits.

A penguin cannot sign a contract in the ordinary legal sense, and it cannot be assumed to understand its contents.

A footprint in ink can look quite official, but it does not solve the problem.

The Question of Experience

One could argue that all these are initial difficulties, and that with suitable training the penguins would gain experience.

The argument is not sufficient.

Training can improve performance in an area that the creature is capable of learning and carrying out. It does not eliminate basic anatomical and cognitive limitations. There is no reasonable number of production-management workshops at the end of which a penguin will grow fingers.

Learning socially from older penguins does not solve this either if there is no experienced penguin who has managed a solar water heater factory in the first place.

Possible Advantages

For the sake of fairness, the advantages should also be noted.

Penguins are social animals. Many species live in large colonies, recognize mates or offspring within a dense group, and cooperate in at least some activities of their lives. They are also able to function in very harsh environmental conditions.

These are admirable qualities.

They are simply not the decisive qualities in running a solar water heater factory.

Excellent swimming ability is not especially useful in the accounting department, either.

Conclusion

The question is not whether penguins are successful animals. They are very successful as penguins.

The question is whether their overall set of skills suits the management of an industrial facility that produces thermal systems for human use.

There is no good reason to think so.

To enable them to do the job, the machines, control interfaces, work environment, communication systems, safety procedures, and organizational structure would all have to be changed, while humans would still have to handle most of the tasks requiring language, documentation, engineering judgment, and legal responsibility.

At that point, the advantage of putting penguins in charge of the factory is not clear.

Therefore, until new data are obtained, it is recommended that solar water heater factories be run by humans.

The penguins are better left in roles where the fact that they are penguins is an advantage.
