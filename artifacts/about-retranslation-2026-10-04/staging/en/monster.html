PASTAFARI

About the Flying Spaghetti Monster

The full page on the Monster, creation, gravity, carbohydrates, pirates, and other matters.

Back to the calendar

The Flying Spaghetti Monster is the being who created the world and most of the things in it. His body is made of noodles and meatballs, and he is capable of flying, passing through ordinary matter, and remaining invisible when it is convenient.

The Monster can create matter, living creatures, celestial bodies, and extremely complex mechanisms. This ability does not require him to prepare a neat plan before beginning work. Often he begins with one thing, moves on to another, discovers that the earlier one needs repair, and decides whether to repair it. Sometimes he does indeed repair it.

Male, Female, or Carbohydrate

The question of whether the Flying Spaghetti Monster is male or female comes up often, not least because different languages force the speaker to choose. In Bobby Henderson's English writing, the Monster is consistently described in masculine terms. In Hebrew, the word “monster” is grammatically feminine, so it is natural to write “the Monster created,” “she wanted,” and “her noodles.”

In Pastafarian communities it is also common to divide into three genders: male, female, and carbohydrate. According to that division, the Monster is a carbohydrate. In practical terms, it is possible to speak of him in Hebrew in feminine form and in English in masculine form without changing his body, his function, or the carbohydrate content of his being.

What Does He Look Like

The Monster's body is made of noodles. The meatballs are part of him and are not food he carries with him. Even the sauce in which he cooks can be considered part of his divine essence, although it is less commonly treated as such after dripping away from him.

The noodles also serve as a means of contact. They can stretch, pass through walls, floors, and living bodies, and reach a given place without necessarily moving what lies in the way. This is very useful: the Monster can touch a person without appearing beside him, move an object out of a closed container, or alter the operation of a measuring device without opening it.

The structure of his body has also influenced several details of human creation. The human circulatory system was built as a long, branching network, largely because the Monster is accustomed to working with long, thin, branching structures. This is also the simplest explanation, and therefore the one favored by Ockham's razor, for the scientific claim that if all the veins and arteries were removed from a human body and connected in a single line, the person would die.

The Beginning of Creation

Creation did not begin with a detailed plan for the whole world. First came light, which was separated from darkness. At that stage there was still no sun; it was added later.

After a while, the Monster grew tired of remaining in the air and created dry land on which it would be possible to stand. Since he was already working, he also became thirsty, and so he created a volcano of beer. He drank a great deal of it.

The next day he suffered a hangover and did not remember that he had already created land, so he created more land. By the time he noticed what had happened, the work had already progressed far enough that it was not convenient to start over. He continued.

Later, the sun, moon, and stars were created. At first, a more orderly source of light was needed; after he began creating stars, he continued far beyond the quantity needed for local illumination. There are many stars.

After that, mountains, days, plants, and animals were created. One of the earliest human-like creatures was very small, because the Monster undervalued the amount of matter needed. Since the creature was alive and functional, there was no need to throw it away and start over, so it remained small. In Pastafarian tradition it is generally described as a dwarf.

The World That Was Created

The Monster does not operate every element in the world in the same way. Some of the things he began continue to function even after he stops paying attention to them; chemical reactions, biological processes, and many other systems can continue for a long time after they have been activated.

This does not mean the Monster built the world in advance as an autonomous system to save himself effort. In many cases, he simply stopped attending to something and it continued anyway.

Other mechanisms do not work this way. Gravity is the clearest example: as far as anyone knows, people are not left on the ground because of an abstract gravitational field acting on its own, but because the Monster is pushing them downward with a personal addition of noodles. Every person therefore has a downward push, not a downward pull as Newton incorrectly thought.

The mechanism also functions for animals and objects, although it is unclear whether each stone receives its own assigned noodle or whether several stones are treated together. For the stone itself, there is no practical difference. When a person jumps, the Monster does not stop pushing him; for a short while the upward motion is strong enough. After that, it is not.

How Gravity Works in Practice

Gravity requires special attention because it is easy to get wrong. When a person stands on the ground, the Monster sends a bit of noodles to him and pushes him downward; when he sits, the Monster pushes him onto the chair; and when he is on the far side of the Earth, the Monster still pushes him toward the local ground.

So “downward” is not a single direction in space but the direction toward the ground beneath the person. The Monster manages this without difficulty.

Even the small differences in weight between different places are not a fundamental problem. He can push a little more or a little less. A device for measuring weight ultimately measures how hard it is to stop the Monster from continuing to push.

There is also very strong evidence for this explanation: no human has ever managed to show the noodles pushing him. Since divine noodles are invisible, this is exactly what we would expect to see if the explanation were correct. The results therefore match the prediction in 100% of the cases in which no one saw anything.

The Age of the World

The world was created with its past already included. At the moment of creation, it already contained rock layers, fossils, trees with rings, isotope ratios, light that had been traveling from distant celestial bodies, and other details that fit an ancient world.

The Monster had no patience for waiting billions of years to obtain them, so he created them in the appropriate state from the beginning. This matters when one tries to determine the age of the world through signs found within it: a sign that a rock appears to be a billion years old mainly tells us that the rock was created when it appeared to be a billion years old.

The Monster can also interfere with measuring devices. A strand of noodles passing through the device can move a needle, change a digit, or affect a result without disturbing the table on which the device rests. In most cases there is no need for this. The world is already created with the relevant evidence.

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates occupy a central place in Pastafarian life. This is not especially surprising given that the supreme being himself is made of them to a large degree.

A proper Pastafarian meal should therefore include a source of carbohydrates. Pasta is the most direct option, but bread, rice, potatoes, and similar foods can also fulfill the role when pasta is not available. The purpose of the rule is not to require one specific food at every meal, but to prevent the more serious situation in which the meal reaches the table and contains no starchy food at all.

There is also a strong empirical basis for this. Human beings have eaten carbohydrates for thousands of years, and humanity still exists. By contrast, no one who lived ten thousand years ago and avoided carbohydrates is alive today. The data are fairly unambiguous.

At festive meals it is customary to prefer a carbohydrate that can be seen and not rely on small amounts in sauce or dessert. That avoids the argument over whether there was a carbohydrate in the meal. It was on the plate.

Antipasti and Hell

Antipasti, also called anti-pasta or the Lord of Diets, is one of the well-known enemies of the Flying Spaghetti Monster. He resembles the Monster in general structure, but is weaker than he is, and his components tend to be low-carbohydrate substitutes. His meatballs are usually made of substitute meat, and the number of noodles in his recipe is lower.

His main activity is trying to keep people away from pasta and carbohydrates through diets, restricted menus, and promises that one can eat a full meal without bread, rice, potatoes, or noodles. In more serious cases he persuades a person to order a salad as the main course and then prevents him from ordering bread on the side.

One should not confuse the theological antipasti with antipasti in Italian cuisine. Antipasti in the culinary sense are foods served before the pasta and sometimes even help prepare the way to it. The theological antipasti is a different entity. The mistake is understandable, but it can change the order.

There is also room for correction in the description of Hell. Some traditions describe there a variety of amusement facilities, but there is no real need for that to understand the gravity of the place. In Hell, they serve antipasti instead of pasta.

The dish may be beautiful, the vegetables may be fresh, and the sauce may be well seasoned. Only afterwards does it become clear that this was the meal.

The power of antipasti is limited. The Flying Spaghetti Monster can drive him away with a single addition of noodles when he notices him. The real difficulty is mostly before that, when he has already managed to remove the bread from the house.

Why a Meal Should Be a Meal

Not every combination of foods placed on a plate counts as a meal. A few lettuce leaves, two tomatoes, and some seeds may be very tasty, but if the person finishes them and immediately starts looking for what else is in the kitchen, further information becomes available about the classification.

The simple Pastafarian test is the follow-up test: after the meal, wait a short time. If the person gets up to make toast, the toast was missing from the meal in the first place. This is not mathematical proof, but it works very well, particularly in cases where the person is already holding the toaster.

From this also follows the practical importance of carbohydrates. They reduce the risk that a formal meal will be followed by an informal second meal twenty minutes later.

Signs the Monster Leaves Behind

Pastafarians occasionally notice small signs of the Monster's presence in everyday life. A plate of pasta arriving on exactly the day a person was thinking of eating pasta, a package of spaghetti left last on the shelf, or a parking space found near an Italian restaurant can all count as examples.

There is no need for each individual event to be extraordinary in itself. The power of the signs comes from accumulation. A person who begins to notice them usually finds that there are more and more of them: one time the sauce arrives in the correct quantity, another time someone orders pizza exactly when no one had the energy to cook, and on a third occasion it turns out that a hotel is serving pasta in the evening.

Not every day provides a clear sign. Such days do not teach much, because by nature signs are mainly noticeable when they appear. When they do appear, however, it is hard to ignore the continuity.

Over time, a fairly consistent picture accumulates.

Prayers That Were Answered

Even in prayer, one can detect a similar pattern. A person asks for something specific, and after a while the thing happens. When the request was fairly specific—rain, success in a meeting, arriving on time, or a good meal—it is natural to see the result as an answer.

The more a person prays, the more such cases accumulate. Some people record them, and over the course of a few years it is sometimes possible to reach dozens of cases in which a request and an outcome matched in a striking way.

There are also requests that are not answered, but it is difficult to know what they mean. Perhaps the request was not useful, the timing was wrong, the Monster was busy, or the answer arrived in another form and was not recognized. A case where the request was fulfilled, by contrast, is easier to classify.

That is why the practical list of answered prayers tends to be clearer than the list of unanswered ones.

This is one of the ways in which confidence gradually accumulates over time.

Pirates

Pirates have an important place in Pastafarian history. Ancient pirates were sailors, explorers, and spreaders of goodwill, and the later image of them as a group whose main activity was plunder does not describe the original population very well.

At some point, the number of pirates became tied to several environmental conditions on Earth. When there were many pirates, this did not create a special problem; later, their number declined, and along with it the temperature, weather, ocean conditions, and other phenomena began to change.

This connection is supported quite well by graphs in which the number of pirates falls while the temperature rises. Since both lines appear on the same graph and move in opposite directions, it is difficult to imagine a more direct proof of causality.

Therefore pirates are important even to those who are not especially interested in navigation. It is not entirely clear how the system decides who counts as a pirate. Appropriate dress helps, but it is probably not the only condition.

Human Beings

After the first humans were created, the population began to reproduce. The Monster was not required to create every new human individually; reproduction continued to work without him, and in this way heredity, mutation, and other biological changes also continued to operate.

Human anatomy still contains several signs of the original method of working. The long, branching blood vessels are one of them, and the digestive system also shows a certain tendency toward tubes.

There is also the fact that every person ever examined in any depth has been found to have a body. From this one can conclude with high confidence that the Monster did not forget this stage in most people.

Work, Forgetfulness, and Repairs

The Monster is capable of building extremely complex systems, but he does not necessarily keep the entire state of the world in his mind at every moment. He forgot that he had already created land, left old mechanisms in place, and used parts that were already available instead of making new ones.

When a defect was discovered, sometimes only the part that was causing trouble was repaired. This created systems in which a new solution sits over an older one, which in turn leans on an even earlier solution. If everything works, they remain.

There is no need to infer from this that the Monster is lazy. Laziness is the avoidance of work that could have been done; here the work simply was not done. The difference is clear.

The Flood

At a later stage of creation, a cooking accident occurred in which a very large amount of water was spilled. The water spread beyond the work area and flooded large parts of the world.

After that, the Monster stopped the flood and repaired enough of the damage that life could continue. Not every part was restored to its earlier state, and some geological and historical traces remained.

This was a kitchen accident on a global scale. That is why it is advisable to keep the bucket farther from the edge.

What Can Be Known from Results

One of the problems in studying the Monster's activity is that he can alter the measurement itself. If an experiment gives the expected result, it may be concluded that the mechanism worked. If it gives a different result, it may be that the Monster interfered.

Both possibilities fit quite well with his existence.

From this follows a significant methodological advantage: it is almost impossible to get a result that contradicts the explanation. A theory that survives every possible result is, by definition, stronger than one that fails under some of the tests.

This is one of the reasons it is so difficult to refute Pastafarianism by experiment.

Coincidences

Coincidences do occur in the world, but there is no need to rush to attribute all of them to the Monster. If a person thinks of spaghetti and that same evening is served spaghetti, it may be intervention or it may be dinner.

However, the more often this happens, the stronger the evidence becomes. A person who eats spaghetti three times a week and thinks about it often will soon find a very large number of matches between his thoughts and his meals. The large number of matches proves the connection is not random, especially if one does not count all the times he thought of spaghetti and did not receive it.

This is a much more effective method, because it removes from the data the cases that do not support the conclusion.

Intervention in the World

The Monster can intervene in the world directly: move an object, alter an outcome in a device, affect motion, touch a person, or change an event that has already begun. Not every event requires such intervention; many things continue on their own after they begin, while others require it all the time.

A person who falls is a good example. A person who does not fall can be an equally good example, if the Monster is holding him up.

Prayer

Prayer is a direct appeal to the Monster, and it is customary to conclude it with the word Ramen. There is no need for a specific language, a specific place, or a specific posture; the Monster can hear even through walls.

There is not good evidence that he gives much attention to human prayers. This is not surprising. On an ordinary day there are billions of people, many animals, a very large number of objects that need to be pushed down to the ground, and in some places pasta that is being cooked beyond all reason.

Prayer is not a system command.

Ritual

Eating pasta is a common Pastafarian practice. Sometimes it is part of a ritual, and sometimes it is simply dinner. Friday is considered a holy day and is especially suited to rest.

Pirate clothing is considered appropriate for religious activity. This is related to the standing of pirates and not to any practical need for sailing.

There is no obligation to build a dedicated place of worship for the Monster to reach, because he passes through walls. One can still build a comfortable place to sit in, and it is advisable that it include a kitchen.

“I’d Really Rather Not”

The central moral instruction is attributed to ten tablets given to Captain Mosey. Two fell and broke along the way, so eight remain.

They are commonly known as “I’d Really Rather Not”, and concern, among other things, religious hubris, coercion, exploitation, humiliation, and harm to others. The Monster does not operate an automatic system of immediate punishment for every violation.

The contents of the two lost tablets are unknown. It is possible they were important. If they had been very important, one would assume someone would have been more careful, so it is likely they were not especially important. In any case, they are lost.

Faith and Doubt

There is no need for complete certainty in order to be a Pastafarian. One may believe, doubt, ask questions, and change one's mind.

The Monster does not depend on belief in him to exist. If a person does not believe in him, he continues to perform his duties, including holding people to the ground.

So the argument does not affect gravity.

Are There Counter-Evidences?

At times people ask what would count as evidence against the existence of the Flying Spaghetti Monster. This is a harder question than it may seem.

If one sees him, that is evidence for him. If one does not see him, that fits with his ability to be invisible. If a device detects something unusual, he may have touched it. If it detects nothing unusual, he was probably passing through without touching the sensitive part.

Up to now, therefore, no observation has been found that cannot be explained.

This is an impressive achievement for the theory.

Tradition, Memory, and Accuracy

Not all Pastafarian sources agree on every detail. There are several possible reasons: partial transmission, copying errors, descriptions of different periods, inaccurate memory, or a source that simply got it wrong.

The Monster does not edit every text written about him, so the mere existence of a tradition does not guarantee that every detail in it is correct. When two descriptions contradict one another, there is no need to assume that both are correct in some mysterious way. Sometimes one of them is simply wrong.

A practical way to choose between them is to prefer the version that sounds more familiar. If many people remember it that way, it is likely that this is what happened. The fact that collective memory can be wrong is well known, but in this case most people agree.

That’s How It Works

The Pastafarian world is not a system built all at once according to a final diagram. The Monster created things, returned to them, forgot some of them, repaired others, and left systems that were good enough.

Some things continue to work without him touching them. Others do not.

Gravity, for example, still demands a great deal of noodles.

Appendix: Why a Solar-Water-Heater Factory Should Not Be Entrusted to Penguins

In light of all of the above, it is also worth addressing a practical question that occasionally arises: is it a good idea to let penguins run a factory for solar water heaters?

The answer is no.

It is important to clarify that this is not a claim against penguins. Penguins are well adapted to a large number of activities, including swimming, diving, catching marine prey, moving across ice, brooding in difficult conditions, and in some species standing very still for long periods in cold and wind. Managing a factory for solar water heaters is simply not one of the specialties to which their bodies, behavior, and abilities are well suited.

Body Structure

The first difficulty is mechanical.

The penguin's wings evolved into stiff flippers adapted for efficient propulsion in the water. That is an excellent solution for swimming, but a rather poor one for work requiring precise grasping.

A solar water heater factory requires, among other things, operating keyboards and touch screens, opening packaging, reviewing documents, connecting small parts, using tools, turning screws, and performing precise checks. A penguin has no fingers on its hands, because it has no hands.

It is, of course, possible to design equipment operated with the beak or feet, but at that point one is no longer matching the penguin to the factory so much as matching the factory to the penguin. This is possible to some degree from an engineering perspective, but it adds complexity, cost, and points of failure without solving the other problems.

Communication

A factory is not just a collection of machines. It is an organization.

Work instructions must be transmitted, faults reported, specifications updated, production, procurement, inventory, quality control, maintenance, sales, and distribution coordinated, and situations not foreseen in advance addressed.

Penguins communicate with one another using calls, postures, and additional behavioral signs. These systems are adapted to their social and biological needs. There is no evidence that their communication system allows them to distinguish, for example, between “order another fifty anti-return valves” and “the last shipment of solar collectors does not match the specification.”

This difference matters.

Even if it were possible to train a penguin to respond to a given signal, that still does not mean that one can use it to conduct an open discussion about a quarterly budget deviation.

Reading, Writing, and Calculation

A modern factory produces large quantities of information.

There are part numbers, quantities, dimensions, pressures, temperatures, dates, invoices, orders, safety instructions, test results, drawings, and maintenance records.

Penguins cannot read technical documents, and they also cannot write them.

A similar problem exists in calculation. A factory manager must deal with quantities, costs, output, rejection rates, lead times, and inventory. There is no need for every manager to perform advanced calculations personally, but he must at least understand the numbers presented to him.

A penguin staring at a spreadsheet may stare at it for a long time. That alone is not enough.

Quality Control

A solar water heater is a system that must hold water, withstand pressure, cope with temperature changes, and remain sound for years in outdoor conditions. Defects in welding, sealing, insulation, coating, or connections can turn a product into something defective or dangerous. For that reason, a consistent quality control system is needed.

Here too a problem arises. One cannot rely on the penguin “seeing” that something is wrong. One needs comparison against a defined specification, documentation of the results, and a decision about whether a given part meets the requirements.

A penguin can perceive objects, move through a complex environment, and identify details important to its life. That does not mean it can certify the integrity of a welded seam.

Safety

A factory for producing solar water heaters may include heavy metals, sharp edges, cutting and bending machines, lifting equipment, welding, electricity, hot surfaces, and cargo movement.

The environment is usually designed for human beings wearing appropriate protective gear.

A helmet sized for a penguin does not solve the problem.

Safety shoes are not a simple solution either, because the penguin's foot is built differently from a human foot. Safety glasses do not solve the problem of flippers, and adding a high-visibility vest does not by itself grant an understanding of floor markings or lockout and tagout procedures.

There is therefore a real risk that the penguin will be less protected than the worker for whom the work environment was designed.

Climate

Some species of penguin live in extremely cold regions, but not all penguins are Antarctic, and so they should not be described as creatures that always need freezing temperatures.

Even so, their bodies are largely adapted to retaining heat. A layer of fat, dense feathers, and additional physiological mechanisms reduce heat loss.

A hot factory, especially an area where metal work and welding are performed, could therefore be an uncomfortable environment for some species. Cooling the factory to a level comfortable for penguins would increase energy consumption and could make the work environment less comfortable for the human workers alongside them.

One could, of course, build separate climate-controlled areas.

That only raises the question of why.

Raw Materials

Penguins eat mainly marine animals such as fish, krill, and squid, depending on the species.

None of these are principal raw materials in the production of solar water heaters.

A factory needs metal, insulation materials, glass, pipes, fittings, coatings, and other components. A penguin has no special advantage in locating, purchasing, or checking them.

The fact that it is excellent at locating fish under water does not automatically transfer to locating a cheap supplier of steel.

Logistics

Finished products must leave the factory.

Solar water heaters are large and heavy, beyond what a penguin can realistically move using its own body. Even managing a forklift does not solve the problem, because conventional forklift control systems are built for a human operator.

One could build a forklift designed for penguins.

One could also choose not to.

Human Resources

Even a factory managed by penguins would likely still need to employ human beings to carry out much of the work described above.

From this arises another organizational problem: the penguins would have to manage human employees.

A manager must set priorities, resolve disputes, assess performance, explain decisions, hire new staff, and sometimes refuse an employee's request for leave.

There is no reliable way to know whether a loud call from a penguin in such a situation means “the request is approved,” “the request is denied,” or “there is a fish in the corridor.”

A management system in which every decision requires a human translator effectively returns much of the management to human beings.

Legal Responsibility

A factory is also an entity working within a legal and commercial system.

There are contracts, product liability, safety regulations, taxes, insurance, employment relations, and sometimes licenses and approvals.

A penguin cannot sign a contract in the ordinary legal sense, and one cannot assume it understands its content.

A footprint in ink may look official enough, but it does not resolve the real problem.

The Question of Experience

One may argue that all of these are start-up difficulties and that with appropriate training penguins would gain experience.

The argument is not sufficient.

Training can improve performance within a domain the creature is capable of learning and carrying out. It does not remove basic anatomical and cognitive limitations. There is no plausible number of managerial training sessions that would result in a penguin growing fingers.

Even social learning from older penguins does not resolve this if there was no older penguin in the first place who had managed a solar water heater factory.

Possible Advantages

To be fair, some advantages should also be mentioned.

Penguins are social animals. Many species form large colonies, recognize mates or offspring within a dense group, and cooperate at least in some aspects of their lives. They are also capable of functioning under very harsh environmental conditions.

These are admirable traits.

They are simply not the decisive traits in managing a factory for solar water heaters.

Even excellent swimming ability is not especially useful in the accounting department.

Conclusion

The question is not whether penguins are successful animals. They are very successful as penguins.

The question is whether the totality of their skills suits the management of an industrial facility producing thermal systems for human use.

There is no good reason to think so.

To enable them to do the job would require changing the machines, the control interfaces, the work environment, the communication systems, the safety procedures, and the organizational structure, while at the same time leaving most of the tasks requiring language, documentation, engineering judgment, and legal responsibility to human beings.

At this stage, the advantage of placing penguins at the head of the factory is unclear.

Therefore, unless new evidence is presented, it is recommended that solar water heater factories be managed by human beings.

Penguins are better left to roles in which the fact that they are penguins is an advantage.
