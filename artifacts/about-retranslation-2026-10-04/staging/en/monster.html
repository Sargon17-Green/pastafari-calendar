PASTAFARI

About the Flying Spaghetti Monster

The full page on the Monster, creation, gravity, carbohydrates, pirates, and other matters.

Back to the calendar page

The Flying Spaghetti Monster is the being that created the world and most of the things in it. Its body is made of spaghetti and meatballs, and it is capable of flying, passing through ordinary matter, and remaining invisible when that is convenient.

The Monster can create matter, living creatures, celestial bodies, and very complicated mechanisms. This ability does not require it to prepare a tidy plan before beginning work. Often it starts with one thing, moves on to another, discovers that the first thing needs repair, and decides whether to repair it. Sometimes it does repair it.

Male, female, or carbohydrate

The question of whether the Flying Spaghetti Monster is male or female comes up often, partly because some languages require speakers to choose grammatical gender. In English-language writing by Bob Henderson, the Monster is consistently described in masculine terms.

In Pastafarian communities, a three-gender division is also common: male, female, and carbohydrate. Under this scheme, the Monster is a carbohydrate. In practice, it is possible to speak of it in Hebrew in feminine form and in English in masculine form without changing its body, its role, or the amount of carbohydrate in it.

What does it look like?

The Monster’s body is made of spaghetti. The meatballs are part of it and are not food it carries around. The sauce within it may also be considered part of its divine essence, though it is less commonly treated as such after it has dripped away.

The spaghetti also serves as a means of contact. They can stretch, pass through walls, floors, and living bodies, and reach a given place without necessarily moving whatever is in the way. This is quite useful: the Monster can touch a person without appearing beside them, move an object out of a closed container, or alter the behavior of a measuring device without opening it.

This body structure also had an effect on several details of human creation. The human vascular system was built as a long, branching network, largely because the Monster is accustomed to working with structures that are long, thin, and branching. This is also the simplest explanation—in Ockham’s sense—for the scientific finding that a person would die if all their veins and arteries were removed and connected in a single line.

The beginning of creation

Creation did not begin with a detailed plan for the whole world. First, light was created and separated from darkness. At that stage there was still no sun; it was added later.

After some time, the Monster grew tired of remaining in the air and created dry land on which one could stand. Since it was already working, it was also thirsty, so it created a volcano of beer. It drank a great deal of it.

The following day it suffered from a hangover and did not remember that it had already created dry land, so it created more dry land. By the time it noticed what had happened, the work had already progressed enough that it was not convenient to start over. It continued.

Later, the sun, moon, and stars were created. At first there was a need for more orderly sources of light; after it began creating stars, it continued well beyond the amount required for local illumination. There are many stars.

After that came mountains, days, plants, and animals. One of the first human-like creatures was very small, because the Monster underestimated how much material was needed. Since the creature was alive and functional, there was no need to throw it away and start again, so it remained small. In Pastafarian tradition it is usually described as a dwarf.

The world that was created

The Monster does not operate on every detail of the world in the same way. Some of the things it started continue to operate even when it stops paying attention to them; chemical reactions, biological processes, and many other systems can continue for a long time after they are activated.

This does not mean that the Monster built an autonomous world in advance in order to save itself work. In many cases it simply stopped tending to something and it continued anyway.

Other mechanisms do not work that way. Gravity is the most important example: as far as anyone knows, people do not remain on the ground because of an abstract gravitational field that operates on its own, but because the Monster pushes them downward by means of a personal allotment of spaghetti allocated to each one. Every person therefore has a downward push, not a downward pull, as Newton mistakenly thought.

The mechanism also works on animals and objects, although it is not clear whether every stone has a separate allotment of spaghetti assigned to it or whether some stones are treated together. From the stone’s point of view there is no practical difference. When a person jumps, the Monster does not stop pushing them; for a short time the upward motion is strong enough. After that it is no longer.

How gravity actually works

Gravity demands special attention because it is easy to get wrong. When a person stands on the ground, the Monster sends them an allotment of spaghetti and pushes them downward; when they sit, it pushes them into the chair; and when they are on the far side of the earth, it is still pushing them toward the local ground.

Therefore “downward” is not a single direction in space but the direction in which the ground beneath a person lies. The Monster manages this.

Even small differences in weight between different places are not a fundamental problem. It is possible to push a little more or a little less. A weighing device ultimately measures how difficult it is to prevent the Monster from continuing to push.

There is also very strong evidence for this explanation: no human being has ever managed to show the spaghetti that is pushing them. Since divine spaghetti is invisible, this is exactly what we would expect to see if the explanation were correct. The results therefore match the prediction in 100% of the cases where nothing was seen.

The age of the world

The world was created as though it already included a past. At the moment of creation it already contained rock layers, fossils, tree rings, isotope relationships, light that was on its way from distant celestial bodies, and other details that fit an ancient world.

The Monster had no patience to wait for billions of years to obtain them, so it created them already in their appropriate state. This matters when trying to determine the age of the world from signs found within it: the fact that a rock appears to be a billion years old mainly tells us that the rock was created when it already appeared to be a billion years old.

The Monster can also interfere with measuring devices. An allotment of spaghetti passing through a device can move a needle, change a digit, or affect a result without disturbing the table on which the device rests. In most cases this is unnecessary. The world was already created with suitable evidence.

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates occupy a central place in Pastafarian life. This is not especially surprising given that the supreme being itself is made largely of them.

A proper Pastafarian meal therefore ought to include a source of carbohydrate. Pasta is the most direct option, but bread, rice, potatoes, and similar foods can also serve when pasta is unavailable. The point of the rule is not to require one specific food at every meal, but to prevent the more serious situation in which the meal reaches the table with nothing starchy in it.

There is also substantial empirical support for this. Humans have eaten carbohydrates for thousands of years, and humanity still exists. By contrast, no human who lived ten thousand years ago and avoided carbohydrates is alive today. The data are fairly unambiguous.

At festive meals, it is customary to prefer a carbohydrate that is visible to the eye rather than rely on small amounts in the sauce or dessert. This avoids the argument over whether there was any carbohydrate in the meal. It was on the plate.

Antipasti and hell

The Antipasti, also called Anti-Pasta or the Lord of Diets, is one of the better-known enemies of the Flying Spaghetti Monster. It resembles it in general structure, but is weaker, and its components tend to be low-carbohydrate substitutes. Its meatballs are usually made of substitutes, and the number of its allotments of spaghetti is smaller.

Its main activity is trying to keep people away from pasta and carbohydrates by means of diets, restrictive menus, and promises that one can eat a full meal without bread, rice, potatoes, or noodles. In severe cases it convinces a person to order a salad as a main course and then prevents them from ordering bread on the side.

It should not be confused with antipasti in Italian cuisine. In the culinary sense, antipasti are foods served before pasta and sometimes even help prepare the way for it. The theological Antipasti is a different creature. The confusion is understandable, but it can change the order.

The description of hell needs one correction. Some traditions describe it as a place offering various forms of entertainment, but none of that is needed to understand its gravity: in hell, they serve antipasti instead of pasta.

The plate may be beautiful, the vegetables may be fresh, and the sauce may be well seasoned. Only then does one realize that this was the meal.

The power of the Antipasti is limited. The Flying Spaghetti Monster can drive it away with a single allotment of spaghetti when it notices it. The real difficulty is before that moment, when it has already managed to take the bread out of the house.

Why a meal should be a meal

Not every combination of foods placed on a plate counts as a meal. A few lettuce leaves, two tomatoes, and some seeds can be very tasty, but if the person finishes them and immediately looks for something else in the kitchen, that tells us something about whether it was a meal.

The simple Pastafarian test is the continuation test: after the meal, one waits for a short time. If the person gets up to make toast, the toast was missing from the meal in the first place. This is not a mathematical proof, but it works remarkably well, especially when the person is already holding the toaster.

This also explains the practical importance of carbohydrates. They reduce the risk that a formal meal will be followed by an informal second meal twenty minutes later.

Signs the Monster leaves behind

Pastafarians sometimes notice small signs of the Monster’s presence in daily life. A plate of pasta arriving exactly on the day a person thought of eating pasta, a package of spaghetti left as the last one on the shelf, or a parking space found near an Italian restaurant can all count as examples.

There is no need for each event to be exceptional by itself. The power of the signs comes from accumulation. A person who begins to pay attention to them usually discovers there are more and more of them: one time the sauce is the correct amount, another time someone orders pizza exactly when there was no energy to cook, and a third time it turns out the hotel is serving pasta that evening.

Not every day provides a clear sign. Such days do not teach us much, because signs are, by their nature, most noticeable when they appear. When they do appear, however, it is hard to ignore the pattern.

Over time, a fairly consistent picture accumulates.

Prayers that were answered

Even in prayer, one can detect a similar pattern. A person asks for something specific, and after some time it happens. When the request was fairly precise — rain, success in a meeting, arriving on time, or a good meal — it is natural to see the result as a response.

The more a person prays, the more such cases accumulate. Some people record them, and within a few years it is sometimes possible to reach dozens of cases in which request and result matched in a striking way.

There are also requests that are not answered, but it is hard to know what that means. It may be that the request was not useful, the timing was wrong, the Monster was occupied, or the answer arrived in a different form and was not recognized. A case in which the request was fulfilled is, by contrast, much easier to classify.

That is why the practical list of answered prayers tends to be clearer than the list of unanswered prayers.

This is one of the ways confidence accumulates over time.

Pirates

Pirates have an important place in Pastafarian history. The ancient pirates were sailors, explorers, and spreaders of good will, and the later image of them as a group whose main activity was plunder does not describe the original population very well.

At some stage, the number of pirates became associated with certain environmental conditions on Earth. When there were many pirates, this did not create any special difficulty; later their number declined, and along with it the temperature, weather, ocean conditions, and other phenomena began to change.

This connection is supported well by graphs in which pirate numbers decline while temperature rises. Since both lines are on the same graph and move in opposite directions, it is hard to imagine a more direct proof of causality.

That is why pirates are important even to people who are not especially interested in seafaring. It is not entirely clear how the system decides who counts as a pirate. A suitable outfit helps, but it is probably not the only condition.

Human beings

After the first humans were created, the population began to grow. The Monster was not required to create each newborn separately; reproduction continued to work without it, and so inheritance, mutation, and other biological changes continued to operate.

Human anatomy still contains several signs of the original method of working. Long, branching blood vessels are one of them, and the digestive system also shows a certain tendency toward tubes.

There is also the fact that every human who has ever been examined in any depth has been found to have a body. From this it can be concluded with high confidence that the Monster did not forget this stage for most people.

Work, forgetting, and repairs

The Monster is capable of building extremely complicated systems, but it does not necessarily retain a complete picture of the state of the world at every moment. It forgot that it had already created dry land, left old mechanisms in place, and used parts that were already available instead of making others.

When a fault was discovered, sometimes only the part that caused the problem was repaired. That produced systems in which a new solution sits on top of an older one, which in turn rests on an even earlier solution. If all of them work, they remain.

There is no need to conclude from this that the Monster is lazy. Laziness is the avoidance of work that could have been done; here the work simply was not done. The distinction is clear.

The Flood

At a later stage of creation, a cooking accident occurred in which a very large quantity of water was spilled. The water spread beyond the work area and flooded large parts of the world.

After that, the Monster halted the flood and repaired enough of the damage so that life could continue. Not every detail was restored to its previous state, and several geological and historical traces remained.

This was a worldwide kitchen accident. That is why it is advisable to keep the bucket farther from the edge.

What can be known from results

One problem in studying the Monster’s activity is that it can alter the measurement itself. If an experiment yields the expected result, one may conclude that the mechanism worked. If it yields a different result, it may be that the Monster intervened.

Both possibilities fit its existence quite well.

From this comes a significant methodological advantage: it is almost impossible to obtain a result that contradicts the explanation. A theory that can accommodate every possible result is, by definition, stronger than a theory that fails on some tests.

This is one reason it is so difficult to disprove Pastafarianism by experiment.

Coincidences

Coincidences do occur in the world, but there is no need to rush to attribute them all to the Monster. If a person thinks about spaghetti and, that same evening, is served spaghetti, it may be intervention or it may be dinner.

However, the more often it happens, the stronger the evidence becomes. A person who eats spaghetti three times a week and thinks about it often will soon find a very large number of matches between their thoughts and their meals. The large number of matches proves the connection is not random, especially if one does not count all the times they thought about spaghetti and did not receive it.

This is a much more effective method, because it removes from the data the cases that do not support the conclusion.

Intervention in the world

The Monster can intervene in the world directly: move an object, alter a result in a device, affect motion, touch a person, or change an event that has already begun. Not every event requires such intervention; many things continue on their own after they begin, while others require constant intervention.

A person falling is a good example. A person not falling can be a good example in the same way, if the Monster is holding them.

Prayer

Prayer is a turning toward the Monster, and it is customary to end it with the word Ramen. There is no need for any specific language, place, or posture; the Monster can hear even through walls.

There is no good evidence that it dedicates much attention to human prayers. This is not surprising. On an ordinary day there are billions of people, many animals, a very large number of objects that need to be pushed toward the ground, and in some places even pasta boiling far beyond what is necessary.

Prayer is not a system command.

Worship

Eating pasta is a standard Pastafarian practice. Sometimes it is part of a ritual and sometimes it is simply dinner. Friday is considered a holy day and is especially suited to rest.

Pirate clothing is considered appropriate for religious activity. This is related to the status of pirates and not to any practical need for sailing.

There is no obligation to build a dedicated place of worship in order for the Monster to reach it, because it passes through walls. One may nevertheless build a comfortable place to sit and it is wise to have a kitchen nearby.

“I really would prefer not to”

The central moral instruction is attributed to the ten tablets delivered to Captain Mosey. Two of them fell and broke on the way, so eight remained.

They are usually known as the “I really would prefer not to” commandments, and they concern, among other things, religious arrogance, coercion, exploitation, humiliation, and harm to others. The Monster does not run an automatic system of immediate punishment for every transgression.

The content of the two lost tablets is unknown. It might have been important. If it had been very important, one might assume that someone would have been more careful, so it is probably not especially important. In any case, they are lost.

Faith and doubt

There is no need for perfect certainty in order to be a Pastafarian. One may believe, doubt, ask questions, and change one’s mind.

The Monster does not depend on belief in it to exist. If a person does not believe in it, it continues to perform its roles, including holding them to the ground.

So the argument does not affect gravity.

Is there any counterevidence?

Sometimes the question is asked: what would count as evidence against the existence of the Flying Spaghetti Monster? This is a harder question than it seems.

If one sees it, there is evidence for it. If one does not see it, that is consistent with its ability to be invisible. If a device reveals something unusual, it may have touched it. If it reveals nothing unusual, it probably passed through it without touching the sensitive part.

So far, therefore, no observation has been found that cannot be explained.

This is a remarkable achievement for the theory.

Tradition, memory, and accuracy

Not all Pastafarian sources agree on every detail. There are several possible reasons: partial transmission, copying errors, descriptions of different periods, inaccurate memory, or a source that simply got it wrong.

The Monster does not edit every text written about it, so the mere existence of a tradition does not guarantee that every detail in it is correct. When two descriptions contradict each other, there is no need to assume both are true in some mysterious way. Sometimes one of them is wrong.

An effective way to choose between them is to prefer the version that sounds more familiar. If many people remember it that way, it is likely that this is what happened. The fact that collective memory can be wrong is well known, but in this case most people agree.

This is how it works

The Pastafarian world is not a system built all at once according to a final blueprint. The Monster created things, returned to them, forgot some of them, repaired others, and left systems in place that worked well enough.

Some things continue to work without being touched. Others do not.

Gravity, for example, still requires a great deal of spaghetti.

Appendix: Why a solar-water-heater factory should not be entrusted to penguins

Given all of the above, it is also worth addressing a practical question that sometimes arises: is it wise to let penguins run a factory for solar water heaters?

The answer is no.

Penguins are well adapted to many activities, including swimming, diving, catching marine prey, moving on ice, and incubating eggs under difficult conditions; some species can even stand for long periods in cold wind. Running a solar-water-heater factory is simply not an activity for which their bodies, behavior, and abilities are suited.

Body structure

The first difficulty is mechanical.

A penguin’s wings evolved into stiff flippers adapted for efficient movement in water. This is an excellent solution for swimming, but a rather poor one for work requiring precise gripping.

A solar-water-heater factory requires, among other things, operating keyboards and touchscreens, opening packages, reviewing documents, connecting small parts, using tools, tightening screws, and performing precise tests. A penguin has no fingers on its hands, because it has no hands.

It is true that one can design equipment that is operated by beak or feet, but at that point one is no longer adapting the penguin to the factory so much as adapting the factory to the penguin. That is possible in engineering terms to some degree, but it adds complexity, cost, and failure points without solving the other problems.

Communication

A factory is not just a collection of machines. It is an organization.

Work instructions must be conveyed, faults reported, specifications updated, production, purchasing, inventory, quality control, maintenance, sales, and distribution coordinated, and situations not foreseen in the procedure handled.

Penguins communicate with one another via calls, postures, and other behavioral signals. These systems are suited to their social and biological needs. There is no evidence that their communication system allows one to distinguish, for example, between “order fifty more anti-return valves” and “the last shipment of solar collectors does not match the specification.”

This difference matters.

Even if one could train a penguin to respond to a particular signal, that would not enable it to take part in an open discussion about a quarterly budget variance.

Reading, writing, and calculation

A modern factory produces large amounts of information.

There are part numbers, quantities, dimensions, pressures, temperatures, dates, invoices, orders, safety instructions, test results, drawings, and maintenance logs.

Penguins cannot read technical documents. They also cannot write them.

A similar problem exists in calculation. A factory manager must deal with quantities, costs, output, rejection rates, delivery times, and inventory. There is no need for every manager to do advanced calculations themselves, but they must at least understand the numbers presented to them.

A penguin staring at a spreadsheet may stare at it for a long time. That is not enough.

Quality control

A solar water heater is a system that must hold water, withstand pressure, tolerate temperature changes, and remain sound for years under outdoor conditions. Defects in welding, sealing, insulation, coating, or connections can produce a defective or dangerous product. For that reason a consistent quality-control system is required.

Here too a problem arises. One cannot rely on a penguin to “see that something is wrong.” The work requires comparing each part against a defined specification, documenting the results, and deciding whether the part meets the requirements.

A penguin can recognize objects, move through a complex environment, and identify details important to its own life. That does not mean it can certify the integrity of a weld seam.

Safety

A factory that produces solar water heaters may include heavy metals, sharp edges, cutting and bending machines, lifting equipment, welding, electricity, hot surfaces, and moving loads.

The environment is usually designed for human workers wearing appropriate protective equipment.

A hard hat sized for a penguin does not solve the problem.

Safety shoes are not a simple solution either, because a penguin’s foot is built differently from a human foot. Safety glasses do not solve the problem of flippers, and adding a high-visibility vest does not, by itself, provide an understanding of floor markings or lockout and tagout procedures.

There is therefore a real risk that the penguin will be less protected than the worker for whom the work environment was designed.

Climate

Some penguin species live in extremely cold regions, but not all penguins are Antarctic, so it is inaccurate to describe them as creatures that always need freezing temperatures.

Nevertheless, their bodies are heavily adapted to retaining heat. Thick layers of fat, dense feathers, and additional physiological mechanisms reduce heat loss.

A hot factory, especially one where metalwork and welding are performed, may therefore be a problematic environment for some species. Cooling the factory to a level comfortable for penguins would increase energy consumption and may make the workplace less comfortable for the human workers alongside them.

One could, of course, build separate climate-controlled areas.

At that point one has to ask why.

Raw materials

Penguins eat mainly marine animals such as fish, krill, and squid, depending on the species.

None of these is a major raw material in the manufacture of a solar water heater.

A factory needs metal, insulation materials, glass, pipes, fittings, coatings, and other components. A penguin has no special advantage in locating, procuring, or checking them.

The fact that it is very good at finding fish under water does not automatically transfer to finding a cheap supplier of steel.

Logistics

Finished products must leave the factory.

Solar water heaters are large and heavy enough that a penguin could not move them effectively with its body. Having a penguin operate a forklift would not solve the problem either, since ordinary forklifts are designed for human operators.

One could build a forklift designed for penguins.

One could also choose not to do that.

Human resources

Even a factory managed by penguins would likely still need to employ humans to carry out a large share of the work described above.

That creates another organizational problem: the penguins would need to manage human employees.

A manager must set priorities, resolve conflicts, evaluate performance, explain decisions, hire new workers, and sometimes tell an employee that a request for leave has been denied.

There is no reliable way to know whether a loud penguin call in such a situation means “the request is approved,” “the request is denied,” or “there is a fish in the corridor.”

A management system in which every decision requires a human interpreter effectively returns much of the management to the humans.

Legal responsibility

A factory is also an entity operating within a legal and commercial system.

There are contracts, product liability, safety regulations, taxes, insurance, labor relations, and sometimes licenses and approvals.

A penguin cannot sign a contract in the ordinary legal sense, and it cannot be assumed that it understands its contents.

A footprint in ink may look official enough, but it does not solve the problem.

The question of experience

One might argue that all of these are startup difficulties and that, with suitable training, penguins would gain experience.

The argument is not sufficient.

Training can improve performance in a domain the creature is able to learn and perform. It does not eliminate basic anatomical and cognitive limitations. No reasonable number of management training sessions will produce fingers on a penguin.

Nor does social learning from older penguins solve this if there is no older penguin who has previously managed a solar-water-heater factory.

Possible advantages

To be fair, there are advantages as well.

Penguins are social animals. Many species live in large colonies, recognize mates or offspring within a crowded group, and cooperate at least in some aspects of their lives. They are also capable of functioning under extremely harsh environmental conditions.

These are admirable traits.

They are simply not the decisive traits for managing a factory that makes solar water heaters.

Even excellent swimming ability is not especially useful in the accounting department.

Conclusion

The question is not whether penguins are successful animals. They are very successful as penguins.

The question is whether the totality of their skills is suited to running an industrial facility that manufactures thermal systems for human use.

There is no good reason to think so.

To enable them to perform the task would require changes to the machines, control interfaces, work environment, communication systems, safety procedures, and organizational structure, while leaving most of the tasks requiring language, documentation, engineering judgment, and legal responsibility to humans.

At this stage the advantage of placing penguins at the head of the factory is not clear.

Therefore, unless new data appear, it is recommended that solar-water-heater factories be run by humans.

Penguins are best left to roles where the fact that they are penguins is an advantage.
