## Q01 — Norse mythology example

### Real-world factual premise

The reconstruction says Odin and Thor are powerful divine figures but are not invulnerable, and that Ragnarok traditions include their deaths. It also treats Loki as a supernatural figure associated with the gods, rather than relying on a simplistic modern taxonomy.

This premise is sound.

A current reference from Store norske leksikon states that at Ragnarok:
- Fenrir kills Odin;
- Thor kills the Midgard Serpent and then dies from its poison;
- Heimdall and Loki kill each other.

Reference:
https://snl.no/ragnarok

### Logic

The intended inference is valid only in the limited form:

> the concept “god/divine being” does not logically entail omnipotence or invulnerability.

It does **not** prove anything about the actual powers of the Flying Spaghetti Monster.

### Prior project decision versus future corpus authority

Historical recovery now establishes that the later user-approved material did not merely use Odin/Thor/Loki as hypothetical examples. It required a concrete project-world ontology:
- Odin is real but not omniscient;
- Thor is connected with storms/lightning;
- Loki is a shape-shifter;
- gods can be limited, mistaken, quarrelsome and defeatable.

That is therefore a **previously approved project decision**, not an unresolved historical blank.

The separately developed canonical corpus will nevertheless become the superior authority. The future task is reconciliation: preserve this prior decision unless the corpus supersedes or refines it.

## Q02 — Mechanism versus agent

The reconstruction makes two claims:

A. describing a mechanism does not, by itself, prove that no agent caused/initiated it;
B. the existence of a mechanism does not, by itself, provide evidence that a proposed agent did cause/initiate it.

Both are logically important and the draft currently preserves both directions.

The light-switch analogy is suitable because:
- electrical mechanism and human agency answer different causal-level questions;
- a complete lower-level description can coexist with an intentional action description.

### Risk

Do not let the final edit degrade this into:
- “science explains only how, never who”;
- “because a mechanism exists, an agent must exist”;
- “a physical explanation cannot count against an agency hypothesis”.

All three stronger claims would be wrong.

## Q03 — Wishful thinking / epistemic activism

The reconstruction correctly separates several propositions:

- interpretation can involve active selection rather than passive discovery;
- choosing a preferred interpretation can affect what the chooser believes and does;
- beliefs can influence later reality through behavior;
- this does not retroactively change already-fixed facts;
- self-fulfilling effects are contingent, not guaranteed.

This is internally coherent.

### Terminology

`אקטיביזם אפיסטמי` is project terminology / conceptual framing, not a standard term that should be falsely attributed to academic epistemology.

The exact recovered sentence:

> לכן wishful thinking הוא, במובן הזה, אקטיביזם אפיסטמי גלוי.

should remain tagged internally as exact recovered wording.

### Philosophical risk

The analogy to judicial/interpretive activism is useful only if the text preserves the difference between:
- selecting among genuinely available interpretations;
- inventing an interpretation unconstrained by the text.

A future prose pass should avoid implying that preference alone makes an interpretation textually justified.

## Q08 — Problem of evil reconstruction

The newly reconstructed section is philosophically defensible in its limited form:

- the classical logical/tensional problem of evil depends on a strong package of divine attributes;
- if a deity is not assumed omnipotent, omniscient and perfectly good, the same contradiction pressure does not arise in the same form;
- this does not prove the limited deity exists or that the model is true.

The staging prose correctly says the model needs fewer perfection-preserving rescue assumptions, rather than claiming it solves every philosophical problem of evil.

Canon dependency:
- prior project approval exists;
- final theological status still waits for corpus reconciliation.

## Q09 — Preferred conclusion as “evidence”

The reconstructed section intentionally contains circular/social-proof reasoning.

Its structure is:

1. prefer conclusion C;
2. adopt C;
3. count the fact that someone adopted C as additional support for C.

That is not sound evidence generation in the real editorial process.

It is suitable only as deliberate epistemology satire.

Guard:
- do not let this mechanism leak into provenance or canon selection;
- keep the real project evidence process source/corpus based.

## Q10 — Disorder among traditions

The reconstructed section uses a real but weak heuristic and deliberately pushes it too far:

- independent historical transmission can indeed produce variation/contradiction;
- therefore some messiness may be compatible with genuine transmission;
- but contradiction does not, by itself, establish truth or authenticity.

The comic inference:

> if it were invented, someone would have organized it better

depends on unstated assumptions about how invented traditions are produced.

That makes it suitable as deliberate reasoning material, provided the real provenance process remains separate.
