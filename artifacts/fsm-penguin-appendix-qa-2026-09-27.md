## Verified background facts

### Body and swimming adaptation — PASS

Smithsonian Ocean describes penguins as strongly adapted for swimming, with torpedo-shaped bodies and powerful flippers; their wings function as underwater propulsive structures rather than flight wings.

Source:
https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/seabirds/penguins

The appendix's contrast between swimming-specialized flippers and human industrial hand-tool use is therefore fair as comic exposition. The specific factory-tool examples are editorial, not biological-source claims.

### Penguins do not all live in extreme cold — PASS

Smithsonian explicitly notes that only a minority of penguin species use the Antarctic continent and that penguins inhabit environments ranging from Antarctica to the equatorial Galápagos and the Atacama coast.

Source:
https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/seabirds/penguins

This supports the appendix's corrective sentence:

> לא כל הפינגווינים חיים באנטארקטיקה, ולא כל המינים זקוקים לקור קיצוני.

This is an important accuracy guard. Do not replace it with the common but false “penguins are cold-climate animals” generalization.

### Vocal/visual communication and individual recognition — PASS

Documented penguin communication includes acoustic calls and visual/tactile signals. In multiple species, calls are used to recognize mates, parents and chicks.

Useful references:
- https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Aptenodytes_forsteri/
- https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Eudyptes_schlegeli/
- https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Spheniscus_mendiculus/

The appendix may therefore say that penguins have real communication systems. Its joke depends on **not** equating those systems with human technical/managerial language.

### Solar-water-heating systems really do involve tanks, collectors, heat-transfer components and controls — PASS

U.S. Department of Energy material describes solar water-heating systems with collectors, storage tanks, circulation, heat-transfer fluid/heat exchangers in some designs, and controls/components varying by system.

Sources:
- https://www.energy.gov/eere/buildings/articles/2014-10-14-issuance-test-procedures-and-energy-conservation-standards
- https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/equipment-operations-and-maintenance-summaries

This is enough to support the appendix's general premise that manufacturing/quality control involves technical components and measurements.

## Claims that are reasonable editorial inferences, not directly source-proven facts

These do not need to be removed, but should not be mistaken for results of penguin cognition experiments:

- penguins cannot read a technical standard;
- penguins cannot operate spreadsheets;
- penguins cannot negotiate with aluminium suppliers;
- penguins cannot conduct employee performance reviews;
- a penguin cannot sign a factory-management contract.

The first four are ordinary extrapolations from the absence of any known human-like literacy/industrial institutions in penguins. The prose is satirical, but if publication requires strict factual sourcing, phrase them as absence-of-evidence / absence-of-known-capability statements rather than claims that a specific experiment proved impossibility.

## Legal paragraph — HOLD FOR JURISDICTIONAL QA

The paragraph under `אחריות משפטית` is directionally obvious but legally broad:

> מערכות משפט אנושיות אינן בנויות בדרך כלל כך שפינגווין יכהן כמנהל מפעל...

Do not promote this sentence to a formal legal proposition without deciding the jurisdiction/scope.

For the article's comic purpose, a safer eventual formulation would describe the practical/legal system as requiring human or legally recognized entities to bear contracts and corporate responsibility, rather than claiming a universal rule across every legal system.

No public edit is made yet.

## Industrial-safety paragraph — PASS AS GENERAL EDITORIAL DESCRIPTION

Statements that factories may include sharp metal, hot surfaces, electricity, vehicles and industrial machinery are ordinary descriptions, not specialized numerical claims.

The PPE sentence is a joke about anthropometric mismatch. It should not be expanded into a technical safety standard unless the article intentionally wants one.

## Logic check

The appendix's central inference is not the same kind of deliberate fallacy used elsewhere in the FSM article.

It is an ordinary task-suitability argument:
- penguins have real strengths;
- those strengths do not supply the manipulative, linguistic, technical, administrative and legal capabilities required for factory management;
- therefore being well adapted as a penguin is not evidence of being well adapted as a factory manager.

The line about lack of prior experience and nuclear-reactor management deliberately illustrates a bad inference:
“no experience because no opportunity” does **not** imply positive suitability.

Keep that distinction intact.
